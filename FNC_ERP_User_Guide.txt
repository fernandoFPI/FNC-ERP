SECTION 1 — GETTING STARTED
================================================================================

What this section covers
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Before you can use any module in the FNC ERP, you need to log in, understand
which company you are working in, and know your way around the screen. This
section walks you through everything you need to do on your first day.


Logging In
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Open a web browser and go to the FNC ERP address your administrator provided.
You will see a login screen asking for your **Email address** and **Password**.

  1. Type your email address in the **Email** field.
  2. Type your password in the **Password** field.
  3. Click LOG IN.

If your password is wrong five times in a row, your account will be locked
automatically as a security measure. You will need to ask a system admin to
unlock it, or wait for the lockout period to expire.

If you have forgotten your password, click FORGOT PASSWORD on the login page.
Enter your email address and click SEND RESET LINK. You will receive an email
with a link that lets you set a new password. This link expires after 15 minutes,
so use it quickly. The system will never tell you whether an email exists in the
system — it always shows a generic success message to protect user privacy.


What MFA Is and How to Set It Up
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MFA stands for Multi-Factor Authentication. It means that after entering your
password, you also need to enter a six-digit code from your phone. This makes
your account much harder to break into, even if someone steals your password.

To set up MFA:
  1. Go to SETTINGS in the left sidebar.
  2. Click SECURITY.
  3. Click ENABLE TWO-FACTOR AUTHENTICATION.
  4. A QR code will appear on screen. Open an authenticator app on your phone
     — Google Authenticator and Microsoft Authenticator both work.
  5. In the app, choose "Scan QR code" and point your camera at the screen.
  6. The app will start showing a six-digit code that changes every 30 seconds.
  7. Type the current six-digit code into the **Verification code** field.
  8. Click CONFIRM. MFA is now active.

From this point forward, every time you log in you will be asked to enter your
six-digit code after your password. If you lose access to your authenticator
app, ask a system admin to reset your MFA. They can do this from the Admin
Users page.


Switching Between the Three FNC Companies
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FNC Group has three legal entities, each with its own set of books, employees,
and projects:

  • Nishtimani Yakam — the main contracting and EPC entity
  • Nishtimani Factory — the manufacturing and production entity
  • Al Watanyia — the equipment rental entity

When you first log in, the system will load the company you were assigned to.
You can see which company you are currently in at the top of the screen near
your name.

To switch to a different company:
  1. Click the company name in the top bar.
  2. A dropdown will appear showing all companies you have access to.
  3. Click the company you want to switch to.
  4. The page will reload and all data shown will be from the new company.

This is called the entity switcher. It is critically important. If you raise
a purchase order while in the wrong company, that PO belongs to the wrong
entity and cannot easily be moved. Always check which company you are in before
creating any record.

You will only see companies that a system admin has given you access to. If
you cannot see a company you need, contact your system admin.


Understanding the Screen Layout
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The screen is divided into three main areas:

  LEFT SIDEBAR — This is the navigation menu. Click any item to go to that
  module. Some items have sub-menus that open when you click them. The sidebar
  can be collapsed by clicking the arrow button at the top to give you more
  screen space.

  TOP BAR — This shows your current company, a notifications bell icon, and
  your profile picture or initials. Click the bell to see recent system
  notifications. Click your name to access your profile settings or log out.

  MAIN AREA — This is where all your work happens. Every page, form, and list
  appears here.


Changing Your Theme
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The FNC ERP supports four visual themes to suit your preference and lighting
conditions:

  • Dark Glass — dark background with a frosted glass effect
  • Dark White — dark background with white text
  • Light Glass — light background with glass effect
  • Light Flat — clean light background, no effects

To change your theme:
  1. Click your name in the top right corner.
  2. Click APPEARANCE or go to SETTINGS → APPEARANCE.
  3. Select your preferred theme by clicking it.
  4. The change takes effect immediately.


Finding Your Payslips
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
If you are an employee, you can view and download your own payslips without
needing to contact HR:
  1. Click PAYROLL in the left sidebar.
  2. Click MY PAYSLIPS.
  3. Click any payslip row to view the full breakdown.
  4. Click DOWNLOAD PDF to save a copy.

Only your own payslips are visible here. HR admins and company admins can see
all employee payslips from the main Payroll module.

SECTION 2 — PROJECTS
================================================================================

What this module is for
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The Projects module is where FNC tracks every construction, EPC, or engineering
project from the moment a client brief is received to the moment the work is
approved and complete. It connects directly to procurement (the purchase orders
raised for the project), finance (the costs incurred and invoices raised against
the client), and HR (the staff assigned to work on it). Every cost that touches
a project flows through here, giving management a real-time picture of project
profitability.


Who uses this module
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • Project Manager — creates projects, manages stages, moves the project
    through its lifecycle, adds team members, tracks costs
  • Finance team — views project costs, raises invoices against the project
    from the Finance module
  • Company Admin — can approve projects, view all projects across the entity
  • System Admin — can override any action on any project in any company
  • All staff — can view projects they are members of


The statuses explained
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Status              | What it means                        | How you get here                        | What happens next
--------------------|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|------------------------------------------
pending             | Created but not yet active           | Project Manager creates the project     | Submit for approval or put on hold
submitted           | Sent to management for review        | Click SUBMIT FOR APPROVAL               | Company admin approves or rejects
approved            | Management has signed off            | Company admin clicks APPROVE            | Project Manager clicks START PROJECT

  approval          |                                      | project (requires reason)               |


Key concepts you need to understand
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PROJECT STAGE vs PROJECT STATUS
The project status tells you where the project is in its overall lifecycle
(pending, ongoing, completed, etc.). A project stage is a specific phase of
work within the project — for example, "Foundation", "Structural Frame",
"MEP Fit-Out". Stages help the team track internal progress. A project can be
'ongoing' while its individual stages move from 'pending' to 'active' to
'completed' independently.

PROJECT VALUE vs BUDGET vs ACTUAL COSTS
  • Project value — the contract amount FNC will invoice the client
  • Project budget — the internal estimate of what it should cost to deliver
  • Actual costs — what FNC has actually spent so far (from POs, payroll, MOs)
The difference between the project value and the actual costs is your gross profit.

RFQ NUMBER AND RECEIVING DATE
When a client sends FNC a request for quotation (an RFQ), the document has a
reference number. Record this in the **RFQ number** field when creating the
project so the document can be traced later. The **Receiving date** is the date
FNC actually received the RFQ.

RESPONSIBLE COMPANY
This is which FNC entity owns and executes the project — Nishtimani Yakam,
Nishtimani Factory, or Al Watanyia. Always confirm this before creating the
project, as costs and revenues will be recorded in that entity's books.

WHY INVOICING IS NOT A PROJECT STATUS
The finance team raises invoices from the Finance → Accounts Receivable section,
not by changing the project status. The project status tracks delivery progress.
Invoicing tracks billing and payment. They are parallel processes that happen at
the same time — a project can be 'ongoing' and have multiple invoices issued.


Step by step — creating and running a project
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CREATING A NEW PROJECT
  1. Go to PROJECTS in the left sidebar.
  2. Click NEW PROJECT in the top right.
  3. Fill in the required fields:
       • **Project name** — the client-facing name
       • **Project type** — EPC, construction, manufacturing, etc.
       • **Client name** — the client's company or individual name
       • **Project manager** — select from the employee dropdown
       • **Responsible company** — which FNC entity owns this project
       • **RFQ number** — the client's reference if available
       • **Project value** — the contract amount in the selected currency
       • **Expected start date** and **Expected end date**
  4. Click SAVE. The project is created in 'pending' status.
     A unique project code is generated automatically (e.g., YAK-2025-0042).

STARTING A PROJECT
  5. Open the project. Click SUBMIT FOR APPROVAL.
     Status moves to 'submitted'.
  6. A company admin or system admin opens the project and clicks APPROVE.
     Status moves to 'approved'.
  7. The project manager opens the project and clicks START PROJECT.
     Status moves to 'ongoing'.

ADDING TEAM MEMBERS
  8. Inside the project, open the TEAM tab.
  9. Click ADD MEMBER.
  10. Select the employee, enter their **Role on project**, **Allocated hours**,
      and the dates they will be working on it.
  11. Click SAVE.

CREATING STAGES
  12. Open the STAGES tab inside the project.
  13. Click ADD STAGE.
  14. Enter the **Stage name** and the planned start and end dates.
  15. Click SAVE. The stage starts in 'pending' status.
  16. When work begins on a stage, click the stage row and update its status
      to 'active'.
  17. Update the **Completion %** as work progresses — drag or type in a
      percentage from 0 to 100.
  18. When a stage is fully done, click COMPLETE STAGE. Status moves to
      'completed' and the percentage locks at 100%.

SUBMITTING TO THE CLIENT
  19. When delivery milestones are ready, click SUBMIT TO CLIENT on the project.
  20. This records the submission date in the system.

RECORDING CLIENT APPROVAL
  21. When the client formally accepts the work, click RECORD APPROVAL.
  22. Enter the approval date and any reference number.

COMPLETING A PROJECT
  23. Before you can complete a project, the system checks four things:
        • All project stages must be either 'completed' or 'cancelled'
        • All purchase orders linked to this project must be 'completed' or
          'deleted' (no open POs)
        • All manufacturing orders must be 'completed' or 'cancelled'
        • All contracts must be either 'completed' or 'cancelled'
  24. If any blockers exist, the system will show them. Resolve each one.
  25. Once all blockers are cleared, click COMPLETE PROJECT.
      Status moves to 'completed' and the completion date is recorded.

PUTTING A PROJECT ON HOLD
  26. Click HOLD PROJECT at any point during the lifecycle.
  27. Enter the **Hold reason** — this is mandatory and will be visible in
      the audit trail.
  28. Status moves to 'on_hold'. To resume, click RESUME PROJECT.

CANCELLING A PROJECT
  29. Click CANCEL PROJECT.
  30. Enter the **Cancellation reason** — mandatory.
  31. If the project was already approved or ongoing, status moves to
      'cancelled_after_approval'. Otherwise it moves to 'cancelled'.
  32. Cancelled projects cannot be reopened.

What happens automatically
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • When a project is created, the system generates a unique project code
    in the format [ENTITY PREFIX]-[YEAR]-[SEQUENCE NUMBER].
  • When a project is linked to a contract with an analytic account, costs
    from purchase orders, manufacturing orders, and payroll are automatically
    attributed to that project's cost tracking.
  • When a stage is completed, the system records the actual end date.
  • Project members' overtime records, if linked to the project, feed into
    the project's HR cost summary.


Important rules
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • A project cannot be completed if it has open purchase orders.
  • A project cannot be completed if it has active manufacturing orders.
  • A project cannot be completed if any stages are not yet completed or
    cancelled.
  • Cancellation requires a written reason that is stored permanently.
  • Once a project is cancelled, it cannot be reactivated.
  • A project manager must be assigned before a project can be approved.
  • The project value currency and the contract value currency can differ —
    FX rates are applied automatically when calculating profitability in IQD.


Connections to other modules
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • PURCHASE ORDERS — POs can be linked to a project at creation. They appear
    in the project's PO tab and their costs feed into the project cost summary.
  • MANUFACTURING ORDERS — MOs linked to the project appear in the MO tab.
    Their actual production costs feed into project actuals.
  • FINANCE (AR) — Project invoices raised from the Finance module link back
    to this project. You can see all invoices and payment status in the project's
    Finance tab.
  • INVENTORY — Material issues drawn from warehouse stock for this project
    are tracked in the Materials tab.
  • HR / PAYROLL — Staff assigned to the project have their hours and payroll
    costs attributed to project cost tracking.
  • REPORTING — The Project Profitability report reads directly from project
    data and shows margin per project.

SECTION 3 — PURCHASE ORDERS
================================================================================

What this module is for
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The Purchase Orders module manages every external purchase FNC makes — from the
initial request through stock checking, vendor pricing, internal approval, and
finally goods receipt. It is designed to ensure that FNC never buys something
that is already sitting in its warehouse, that all prices are verified before
commitment, and that no payment can be made without proper management approval.
This is the most process-intensive module in the system.


Who uses this module
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • Organizer — the person who needs something bought. Creates the PO, drives
    it through the process, and reviews all pricing before requesting approval.
  • Store Keeper — checks whether the requested items already exist in FNC's
    warehouses before going to external vendors.
  • Store Pricing Officer — if items are available internally, sets the
    internal transfer price for those items.
  • Procurement Officer — contacts external vendors, collects quotes, and
    records the market prices in the system.
  • 2nd Procurement Officer — independently verifies the market prices
    collected by the Procurement Officer. This double-check prevents pricing
    errors.
  • Department Head — the manager who gives final approval for the purchase.
  • PO Admin — assigns which employees hold which roles for each project or
    department. Must set up positions before the workflow can start.
  • System Admin — can see all POs across all companies, override any stage,
    and approve directly.

CRITICAL CONCEPT — PO ROLES ARE NOT JOB TITLES
PO roles are not the same as an employee's job title in HR. They are position
assignments that a PO Admin configures per project or per department. The same
person could be the Store Keeper for all projects in Yakam but have no PO role
at all in Watanyia. Before the PO workflow can work on a new project, the PO
Admin must go to PO POSITIONS and assign the five roles for that project.


The statuses explained
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

-------------------|-----------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|------------------------------------

inventory_check    | Store Keeper reviewing available stock        | Organizer submits                      | Store Keeper marks check complete

completed          | Goods received and reconciled                 | All items received, Organizer completes| No further action — PO is closed
deleted            | Cancelled before commitment                   | Organizer or Admin deletes             | No further action


Step by step — the full PO journey
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BEFORE YOU START — POSITION SETUP
A PO Admin or System Admin must first assign the five PO roles for the project
or department. If this has not been done, the workflow cannot progress past the
'opening' stage.
  1. Go to PROCUREMENT → PO POSITIONS.
  2. Click ASSIGN POSITION.
  3. Select the **Project** or **Department** this assignment applies to.
  4. Select the **Role** (Store Keeper, Store Pricing, Procurement Officer,
     2nd Procurement, Department Head).
  5. Select the **Employee** who will hold this role.
  6. Click SAVE. Repeat for all five roles.

STEP 1 — CREATING THE PO (THE ORGANIZER)
  7. Go to PROCUREMENT → PURCHASE ORDERS.
  8. Click NEW PURCHASE ORDER.
  9. Fill in:
       • **Project** — link to a project (optional but recommended)
       • **Currency** — the currency the vendor will invoice in
       • **Expected delivery date**
       • **Notes** — any relevant context for the reviewers
  10. Add line items. For each item click ADD LINE and enter:
       • **Description** — what is being purchased
       • **Product** — select from the product catalogue if it exists
       • **Quantity ordered**
       • **Unit of measure** (metres, kg, units, etc.)
       • **Estimated unit price** — your best estimate at this stage
  11. Click SAVE. The PO is created in 'opening' status with an auto-generated
      PO number (e.g., PO-2025-0187).
  12. Review your lines, then click SUBMIT FOR INVENTORY CHECK.
      Status moves to 'inventory_check'.

STEP 2 — INVENTORY CHECK (THE STORE KEEPER)
  13. The Store Keeper sees this PO in their MY PO QUEUE.
  14. Open the PO. For each line, check whether the items are available in
      the company's warehouses.
  15. If items ARE available: note this in the line and mark the line as
      available from stock.
  16. If items are NOT available: mark the line as requiring external purchase.
  17. Click COMPLETE INVENTORY CHECK. Status moves to 'store_pricing'.

STEP 3 — STORE PRICING (STORE PRICING OFFICER)
  18. The Store Pricing Officer sees this PO in their MY PO QUEUE.
  19. For each line marked as available internally, enter the **Store price**
      — what it would cost to transfer from FNC's own stock.
  20. Add any relevant notes about the internal stock condition.
  21. Click COMPLETE STORE PRICING. Status moves to 'market_pricing'.

STEP 4 — GETTING VENDOR QUOTES (PROCUREMENT OFFICER)
  22. The Procurement Officer sees this PO in their MY PO QUEUE.
  23. For each line, contact vendors, collect quotes, and enter:
       • **Vendor** — select the vendor from the vendor list
       • **Vendor quote reference** — the vendor's quotation number
       • **Quoted unit price** — the price the vendor quoted
  24. Click COMPLETE MARKET PRICING. Status moves to 'price_verification'.

STEP 5 — VERIFYING PRICES (2ND PROCUREMENT)
  25. The 2nd Procurement Officer sees this PO in their MY PO QUEUE.
  26. Review all vendor prices. If prices look correct, add **Verification notes**
      confirming the prices are market-accurate.
  27. If prices seem wrong, add notes explaining the discrepancy — the Organizer
      will see these during their review.
  28. Click CONFIRM PRICE VERIFICATION. Status moves to 'review'.

STEP 6 — ORGANIZER REVIEW
  29. The Organizer sees this PO in their MY PO QUEUE.
  30. Review all pricing: store prices, market prices, and verification notes.
  31. If everything is acceptable, click REQUEST APPROVAL.
      Status moves to 'pending_approval'.
  32. If something needs correction, the Organizer can edit the PO at this
      stage and send it back.

STEP 7 — DEPARTMENT HEAD APPROVAL
  33. The Department Head sees this PO in their MY PO QUEUE (or in the
      APPROVAL QUEUE page).
  34. Click ASSIGN TO ME to take ownership. Status moves to 'dept_assigned'.
  35. Review the full PO including all pricing and notes.
  36. If approved: click APPROVE. Status moves to 'approved'. The Organizer
      is notified.
  37. If rejected: click REJECT and enter a reason. The PO is sent back to
      the Organizer for correction.

WHAT HAPPENS WHEN A PO IS REJECTED
  38. The Organizer receives a rejection notification with the reason.
  39. The PO returns to 'review' status.
  40. The Organizer makes the required changes and clicks REQUEST APPROVAL again.
      The cycle repeats from Step 6.

STEP 8 — RECEIVING GOODS
  41. Once the PO is 'approved', place the order with the vendor.
  42. When goods arrive, go to the PO and click RECEIVE GOODS.
  43. A receipt form opens. For each line, enter the **Quantity received**.
      You do not have to receive all items at once — you can receive partial
      quantities across multiple receipts.
  44. Click CONFIRM RECEIPT.
  45. The system automatically creates a stock movement into the warehouse
      and updates inventory balances.

STEP 9 — COMPLETING THE PO
  46. Once all items have been received and reconciled, click COMPLETE PO.
      Status moves to 'completed'.
  47. A completed PO triggers the AP workflow — the Finance team can now
      create a vendor invoice linked to this PO.

DELETING A PO
  • A PO can only be deleted when its status is 'opening' or 'inventory_check'.
  • Once it has progressed beyond inventory check, it cannot be deleted —
    it must be properly completed or cancelled with a documented reason.


My PO Queue — what it is and why you should check it daily
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The MY PO QUEUE page (accessible from PROCUREMENT → MY QUEUE) is personal. It
shows only the POs where you need to take action right now. If you are a Store
Keeper, it shows POs waiting for your inventory check. If you are the Organizer,
it shows POs you need to submit or review. If you are a Department Head, it
shows POs waiting for your approval.

Check this list every morning. If a PO sits waiting for your action, the whole
purchasing process for that project is paused. Delayed POs delay projects.

What happens automatically
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • A unique PO number is generated automatically when the PO is created.
  • When goods are received, the system automatically creates a stock
    movement into the correct warehouse location.
  • When all lines are fully received, the system calculates the PO as
    ready for completion.
  • Approval notifications are sent automatically when status changes
    require another person's action.
  • The Approval Queue count shown in the sidebar badge updates in real time.


Important rules
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • PO roles must be assigned for a project before the workflow can function.
    If the Store Keeper role is not assigned, nobody will receive the PO after
    the inventory check is triggered.
  • You cannot receive more items than were ordered. The system blocks
    over-receipt.
  • A PO cannot be edited after it reaches 'pending_approval' status.
  • Rejection always requires a written reason.
  • A PO linked to a project contributes to that project's cost tracking.
  • The PO currency is set at creation and cannot be changed after the PO
    is submitted.


Connections to other modules
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • PROJECTS — POs created for a project appear in the project's PO tab and
    their costs feed into project financials.
  • INVENTORY — Receiving goods creates an automatic stock movement. The
    inventory balance increases immediately.
  • FINANCE (AP) — A completed PO is the trigger for the Finance team to
    create a vendor invoice. The vendor invoice can be linked to the specific PO.
  • VENDORS — Each PO is linked to a vendor. The vendor's withholding tax rate

SECTION 4 — INVENTORY
================================================================================

What this module is for
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The Inventory module tracks every physical item FNC owns — materials in
warehouses, items on project sites, and goods in transit between locations.
It knows exactly how many units of each product are available, where they are
sitting, what they cost, and when they need to be reordered. Without accurate
inventory, FNC cannot know whether to buy new materials or use what it already
has, and it cannot accurately calculate the true cost of a project.


Who uses this module
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • Store Keeper — manages physical stock, records receipts, performs
    transfers between locations
  • Procurement team — checks stock availability before raising purchase orders
  • Finance team — uses inventory valuations for balance sheet reporting
  • Project Managers — tracks materials issued to projects
  • System Admin — full access to all inventory functions


Key concepts you need to understand
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PRODUCTS AND SKUS
A product is any item FNC tracks in its inventory — steel beams, cables,
generators, screws, paint, etc. Each product has a unique SKU (Stock Keeping
Unit) code. Think of the SKU as the product's identity card. No two products
can share the same SKU within the same company.

STOCK LOCATIONS
Stock can be in different physical places. The system tracks these as locations:
  • Warehouse — a physical storage space (e.g., "Main Erbil Warehouse")
  • Site — a project construction site
  • Virtual In — the system's way of receiving stock from outside FNC
    (goods come in from this virtual source)
  • Virtual Out — the system's way of consuming stock that leaves FNC
    permanently (e.g., materials used in construction and embedded in the work)

STOCK BALANCE — WHAT THOSE NUMBERS MEAN
  • On hand — the total quantity physically present in a location
  • Reserved — quantity committed to a manufacturing order or project issue
    that has not yet been moved
  • Available — on hand minus reserved (what you can actually use or sell)

WHAT AVCO MEANS
AVCO stands for Average Cost Valuation. It is the default way the system
calculates what each unit of stock is worth. Every time new stock arrives at
a different price, the system recalculates the average cost of all units.
Example: You have 10 steel bars at IQD 50,000 each. You receive 10 more at
IQD 60,000 each. The new average cost = (10×50,000 + 10×60,000) / 20 =
IQD 55,000 per bar. This average is used for all subsequent valuations.

STOCK LOTS
A lot (also called a batch) is a group of items that arrived together and are
tracked as a unit. Lots are useful when you need to know the expiry date of a
batch of materials, or when different deliveries of the same product have
different quality certifications. Use lot tracking when traceability matters.

THE IMMUTABLE LEDGER
Every stock movement is permanently recorded and cannot be deleted or changed.
This is by design — just like a financial journal entry, stock moves create a
history that auditors and managers can rely on. If a mistake is made, you
correct it by creating a new counter-movement, not by editing or deleting the
original.


Step by step — common inventory tasks
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ADDING A NEW PRODUCT
  1. Go to INVENTORY → PRODUCTS.
  2. Click NEW PRODUCT.
  3. Enter the **SKU** (must be unique per company), **Name**, **Unit of measure**,
     and **Category**.
  4. Set the **Valuation method** (AVCO is the default and recommended for most
     materials).
  5. Set the **Reorder point** — when stock falls below this number, the system
     will alert you.
  6. Click SAVE.

CREATING A NEW STOCK LOCATION
  1. Go to INVENTORY → LOCATIONS.
  2. Click NEW LOCATION.
  3. Enter the **Location name** and **Code** (must be unique).
  4. Select the **Type** (warehouse, virtual_in, virtual_out).
  5. Click SAVE.

TRANSFERRING STOCK BETWEEN LOCATIONS
  1. Go to INVENTORY → STOCK MOVES.
  2. Click NEW TRANSFER.
  3. Select the **Product**, the **From location**, and the **To location**.
  4. Enter the **Quantity** to move and the **Unit cost**.
  5. Add a lot number if lot tracking is enabled for this product.
  6. Click CONFIRM. The stock balance updates immediately at both locations.

CHECKING CURRENT STOCK LEVELS
  1. Go to INVENTORY → STOCK BALANCES.
  2. Use the filters to search by product, location, or both.
  3. The table shows on-hand, reserved, and available quantities at each
     location.

LOW STOCK ALERTS
When a product's available quantity falls below its reorder point, it appears
highlighted in the stock balances view. Review this regularly and raise purchase
orders to replenish.


What happens automatically
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • When goods are received against a purchase order, the system automatically
    creates a stock move from the Virtual In location to the designated warehouse
    location.
  • When a manufacturing order is completed, the system creates a stock move
    for the finished product into the warehouse.
  • When materials are issued to a project, the system creates a stock move
    from the warehouse to the Virtual Out location.
  • The AVCO unit cost is recalculated automatically every time stock arrives
    at a different price.
  • Inter-company stock transfers are detected automatically and routed to
    the Inter-Company module for pricing approval.


Important rules
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • You cannot move more stock than is available (unless moving from a
    Virtual In location, which has no physical limit).
  • You cannot transfer stock from and to the same location.
  • Stock moves cannot be deleted or edited once created. To correct an error,
    create a counter-transfer in the opposite direction.
  • A product can only be deleted if it has no stock move history. Once a
    product has been traded, it is deactivated (hidden from dropdowns) rather
    than deleted.


Connections to other modules
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • PURCHASE ORDERS — goods receipts create stock movements automatically.
  • MANUFACTURING — component consumption and finished goods output create
    stock movements automatically.
  • PROJECTS — material issues to projects reduce warehouse stock.
  • FINANCE — inventory valuations feed into the balance sheet as assets.
  • INTER-COMPANY — cross-entity stock transfers route through the interco
    module for pricing and GL posting.

SECTION 5 — FINANCE: CORE (ACCOUNTS, JOURNALS, FX RATES, PERIODS)
================================================================================

What this module is for
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The Finance core module is the foundation of FNC's accounting. Every financial
event in the company — every purchase, every payment, every salary, every
invoice — ultimately creates a journal entry here. The general ledger is the
complete, permanent financial record of the company. From it, all financial
reports are generated: the trial balance, profit and loss statement, and balance
sheet.


Who uses this module
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • Finance team — creates and posts journal entries, manages accounts, closes
    periods, monitors FX rates
  • Company Admin — reviews financial reports, closes accounting periods
  • System Admin — full access including period closure and account deletion
  • Other modules — many system actions (payroll posting, vendor payments,
    project invoices) automatically create journal entries without manual input


Key concepts you need to understand
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE CHART OF ACCOUNTS
The chart of accounts is the master list of every financial category the
company uses to record transactions. Every transaction must be assigned to at
least two accounts. The accounts are organised by type:

  • Asset accounts — things FNC owns (cash, receivables, equipment, inventory)
  • Liability accounts — things FNC owes (vendor payables, loans, accruals)
  • Equity accounts — the owners' share of the business
  • Revenue accounts — money earned (project revenue, rental income)
  • Expense accounts — money spent (materials, salaries, depreciation)

WHAT A JOURNAL ENTRY IS (DOUBLE-ENTRY EXPLAINED SIMPLY)
Every financial event must be recorded in at least two accounts. This is called
double-entry bookkeeping. For every amount that goes into one account (a debit),
the same amount must leave another account (a credit). The total of all debits
must always equal the total of all credits. The system enforces this — you
cannot post a journal entry that does not balance.

WHAT DEBIT AND CREDIT MEAN FOR A CONSTRUCTION COMPANY
In everyday language, "debit" means your bank account went down and "credit"
means it went up. In accounting, it is different:
  • Debit increases Asset and Expense accounts
  • Credit increases Liability, Equity, and Revenue accounts
  • The opposite operation decreases the account

Example: FNC pays a vendor IQD 5,000,000 for steel.
  Debit: Materials Expense        5,000,000   (expense goes up)
  Credit: Cash / Bank Account     5,000,000   (asset goes down)
Both lines are for the same amount. The entry balances.

WHY POSTING IS IRREVERSIBLE
A draft journal entry can be edited. Once you click POST, it becomes permanent.
The reason is that the general ledger must be a trustworthy, tamper-proof
record. Auditors need to see that no entry was changed after it was recorded.
If you make a mistake on a posted entry, you must create a reversal entry —
the system can do this automatically when you cancel a posted journal.

WHAT AN ACCOUNTING PERIOD IS
An accounting period is a defined span of time (usually one month) during which
transactions are recorded. FNC uses 12 monthly periods per year. When a period
is closed, no new journal entries can be dated within it. You close a period
when you are confident all transactions for that month have been recorded and
reconciled. Closing is irreversible.

WHAT FX RATE STALENESS MEANS
FNC operates in multiple currencies (IQD, USD, EUR). The system needs to know
the exchange rate to convert foreign-currency transactions into IQD for the
books. If the FX rate for a currency has not been updated recently, the system
flags it as stale. Using a stale rate produces inaccurate financials. The system
will warn you before posting a journal with a stale rate.


Step by step — managing the chart of accounts
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ADDING A NEW ACCOUNT
  1. Go to FINANCE → CHART OF ACCOUNTS.
  2. Click NEW ACCOUNT.
  3. Enter the **Account code** (must be unique per company — e.g., 1001),
     **Account name**, and **Account type**.
  4. Optionally select a **Parent account** to create a hierarchical structure.
  5. Set **Currency** if this account is always in a specific currency.
  6. Click SAVE.

VIEWING THE LEDGER FOR AN ACCOUNT
  1. Go to FINANCE → CHART OF ACCOUNTS.
  2. Click any account row.
  3. Click VIEW LEDGER. You will see every posted transaction on this account,
     with dates, amounts, and running balances.
  4. Use the **From date** and **To date** filters to narrow the view.


Step by step — creating a journal entry
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1. Go to FINANCE → JOURNAL ENTRIES.
  2. Click NEW JOURNAL ENTRY.
  3. Enter:
       • **Entry date** — must fall within an open accounting period
       • **Description** — a plain-English explanation of what this entry records
       • **Reference** — any supporting document reference
  4. Add lines. For each line click ADD LINE and enter:
       • **Account** — select from the chart of accounts
       • **Debit** or **Credit** amount (not both on the same line)
       • **Analytic account** — optional, links cost to a project or dimension
       • **Cost center** — optional, for departmental cost tracking
       • **Currency** — if different from IQD, enter the foreign currency amount
         and the system will look up the current FX rate
  5. The TOTAL DEBITS and TOTAL CREDITS at the bottom must match. If they do
     not match, you cannot post.
  6. Click SAVE to save as draft. Review the entry carefully.
  7. Click POST to make it permanent. The general ledger updates immediately.

CANCELLING A POSTED JOURNAL
  1. Open the posted journal entry.
  2. Click CANCEL.
  3. The system automatically creates a reversal entry with all debits and
     credits swapped.
  4. Both the original and the reversal are kept permanently in the ledger.


Managing accounting periods
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1. Go to FINANCE → PERIODS.
  2. The list shows all 12 monthly periods with their status (open or closed).
  3. To close a period: click the period row, then click CLOSE PERIOD.
  4. The system will block closure if there are any draft (unposted) journal
     entries dated within that period. You must post or delete all drafts first.
  5. Once closed, a period cannot be reopened. Contact a system admin if you
     need to reopen a closed period — this is a rare and serious action.


Managing FX rates
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1. Go to FINANCE → FX RATES.
  2. The page shows all currencies with their latest rates and how old those
     rates are.
  3. Rates highlighted in amber are approaching the staleness threshold.
     Rates highlighted in red are stale and will trigger warnings on journals.
  4. To manually add a rate: click ADD RATE, select the **Currency pair** and
     enter the **Rate** and **Effective date**.
  5. To trigger an automatic sync with the central bank feed, click SYNC RATES.
  6. The system admin can configure the staleness thresholds in Settings.

draft     | Entry created but not yet in the ledger    | Finance user creates entry   | Post or delete
posted    | Permanently recorded in the general ledger | Finance user clicks POST     | Can only be reversed by cancelling

  • VENDOR PAYMENTS — each payment against a vendor invoice creates a GL entry.

SECTION 6 — FINANCE: ACCOUNTS RECEIVABLE (AR)

What this module is for
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Accounts Receivable (AR) is the money that clients owe FNC. When FNC issues a

Who uses this module
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • Finance team — monitors outstanding balances, follows up on late payments,

  • Company Admin and Directors — reviews the AR dashboard for business

  • System Admin — full access


Where AR invoices come from
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
AR invoices in FNC come from two sources:
  1. PROJECT INVOICES — raised by the Finance team against project contracts.
     These are milestone, progress, cost-plus, or fixed lump sum billings to

  2. RENTAL INVOICES — raised automatically when a rental contract billing

You do not create invoices from the AR dashboard. The AR module is a read-only

Understanding the AR dashboard
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1. Go to FINANCE → ACCOUNTS RECEIVABLE.
  2. The dashboard shows four key figures at the top:
       • Total Outstanding — all money currently owed to FNC by all clients
       • Open Invoices — the count of invoices not yet fully paid
       • Overdue Amount — the portion of outstanding that is past its due date
       • Collected This Month — total payments received in the current month

THE AGING ANALYSIS
Below the summary, you will see the aging analysis. This shows how long each
outstanding amount has been owed:
  • 0–30 days — recently issued invoices, not yet overdue (shown in green)
  • 31–60 days — getting close to overdue territory (shown in amber)
  • 61–90 days — significantly overdue, requires active follow-up (shown in red)
  • Over 90 days — seriously overdue, may require legal action (shown in dark red)

The bar length shows what proportion of total outstanding sits in each bucket.
A healthy AR position has most of its balance in the 0–30 day bucket.

THE BY-CLIENT TABLE
Further down the page is a table showing what each client owes. Click any
client row to filter the invoice list below to show only that client's invoices.

THE INVOICE LIST
The invoice list shows every outstanding invoice. You can:
  • Search by invoice number or client name
  • Filter by source (project invoices or rental invoices)
  • Toggle to show only overdue invoices
  • Click any invoice row to navigate directly to the source record (the
    project invoice or the rental contract)

WHAT "OVERDUE" MEANS
An invoice is overdue when today's date is past the invoice's due date and the
invoice has not been fully paid. The due date is set when the invoice is issued,
based on the client's payment terms (e.g., Net 30 means payment is due 30 days
after the invoice date).

IMPORTANT: Payments are recorded in the Projects or Rental module, not from
this AR dashboard. When a payment is recorded there, the AR balance updates
automatically.


How to see what one specific client owes

  2. Scroll to the By Client table.
  3. Click the client's name row. The invoice list below filters to show only
     that client's invoices.
  4. You can see the invoice number, issue date, due date, amount, and
     how many days overdue each invoice is.
  5. Click an invoice row to navigate to the original project invoice or
     rental contract for full details.

SECTION 7 — FINANCE: ACCOUNTS PAYABLE (AP)
================================================================================

What this module is for
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Accounts Payable (AP) is money FNC owes to vendors. When a vendor delivers
goods or services, they send a paper invoice. The Finance team records this in
the AP module as a vendor invoice. Every vendor invoice must go through an
approval process before payment can be made. The AP module tracks all outstanding
vendor invoices, manages approvals, records payments, and shows how long FNC has
owed money to each vendor.


Who uses this module
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • Finance team — creates vendor invoices, records payments, monitors
    outstanding AP balances
  • System Admin — the only role that can approve vendor invoices before
    payment
  • Company Admin — views AP dashboard and aging
  • Management — monitors total outstanding AP for cash flow planning


The difference between a PO and a vendor invoice
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A PURCHASE ORDER (PO) is the document FNC sends to a vendor saying "we want to
buy these items at these prices." The PO is created before goods arrive.

A VENDOR INVOICE is the document the vendor sends to FNC after they have
delivered the goods or services, saying "you owe us this amount." The vendor
invoice is created after goods arrive and the PO is completed.

The Finance team creates the vendor invoice in the system when they receive the
paper invoice from the vendor. The vendor invoice can be linked to the specific
PO that triggered the purchase.


Vendor invoice statuses
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Status          | What it means                             | How you get here                    | What happens next
----------------|-------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|----------------------------------------
draft           | Invoice created but not yet submitted     | Finance user creates invoice        | Add lines, then submit for approval
submitted       | Submitted to system admin for approval    | Finance clicks SUBMIT FOR APPROVAL  | System admin approves or rejects
approved        | System admin approved the invoice         | System admin clicks APPROVE         | Finance can record payments
partially_paid  | Some but not all of the invoice is paid   | Finance records a partial payment   | Continue recording payments
paid            | Fully paid                                | Final payment clears the balance    | Invoice is closed
cancelled       | Voided before payment                     | Finance or admin clicks CANCEL      | No payment can be made


Step by step — creating and paying a vendor invoice
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CREATING A VENDOR INVOICE
  1. Go to FINANCE → ACCOUNTS PAYABLE.
  2. Click NEW VENDOR INVOICE.
  3. Fill in:
       • **Vendor** — select from the vendor list
       • **Purchase order** — optionally link to the PO this invoice covers
         (select from the vendor's completed POs)
       • **Invoice number** — the number printed on the vendor's paper invoice
         (this must match exactly for audit purposes)
       • **Invoice date** — the date on the vendor's paper invoice
       • **Due date** — when payment is due (auto-calculated from the vendor's
         payment terms, but can be overridden)
       • **Currency** — usually matches the PO currency
       • **Cost center** and **Analytic account** — for cost allocation
       • **Notes** — any relevant context
  4. Add invoice lines. For each line click ADD LINE and enter:
       • **Description** — what the line is for
       • **PO line** — optionally link to a specific PO line
       • **Quantity** and **Unit price**
       • **GL account** — the expense account to post this cost to
  5. The totals section shows:
       • Subtotal — sum of all lines
       • Tax — any applicable tax amount
       • WHT — withholding tax (calculated automatically from the vendor's rate)
       • Net payable — what FNC actually owes after deductions
  6. Click SAVE. Invoice is created in 'draft' status.

SUBMITTING FOR APPROVAL
  7. Review the invoice carefully, then click SUBMIT FOR APPROVAL.
  8. Status moves to 'submitted'. System admins are notified automatically.

APPROVAL BY SYSTEM ADMIN
  9. The system admin opens the invoice from their notifications or from the
     AP dashboard (the "Pending Approval" KPI card is clickable and filters to
     submitted invoices).
  10. The system admin reviews all lines and totals.
  11. Click APPROVE to approve. Status moves to 'approved'. The Finance team
      is notified.
  12. If something is wrong: click REJECT, enter the rejection reason, and click
      REJECT INVOICE. The invoice returns to 'draft' and the Finance team is
      notified with the reason.

RECORDING A PAYMENT
  13. Once the invoice is 'approved', open it and click RECORD PAYMENT.
  14. A payment form opens. Fill in:
       • **Amount** — you can pay less than the full amount for partial payments
       • **Payment date**
       • **Payment method** — bank transfer, cheque, cash, or other
       • **Reference** — the bank transfer reference or cheque number
       • **Notes** — any context
  15. Click RECORD PAYMENT.
  16. If the amount is less than the outstanding balance, status moves to
      'partially_paid'. Make further payments until the invoice is fully paid.
  17. When the final payment clears the balance, status automatically moves
      to 'paid'.


What WHT (Withholding Tax) is
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Withholding tax (WHT) is a tax that FNC deducts from vendor payments and pays
directly to the Iraqi tax authority on the vendor's behalf. The vendor invoices
FNC for the full amount, but FNC pays the vendor the net amount (minus WHT) and
remits the WHT separately.

Each vendor has a WHT rate configured in their vendor profile. When you create a
vendor invoice, the system automatically calculates the WHT amount:
  WHT amount = Subtotal × Vendor's WHT rate
  Net payable = (Subtotal + Tax) − WHT amount

You do not need to calculate this manually. The system handles it.


AP aging — what it means
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The AP aging shows how long FNC has owed money to vendors:
  • 0–30 days — invoices recently approved, not yet past their due date
  • 31–60 days — invoices approaching overdue territory
  • 61–90 days — invoices past their due date
  • Over 90 days — seriously overdue, risk of vendor relationship damage

Unlike AR aging (which shows how long clients have owed FNC money), AP aging
shows how long FNC has owed vendors money. Pay invoices before they become
overdue to maintain vendor relationships.


Connections to other modules
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • PURCHASE ORDERS — vendor invoices can be linked to a completed PO for
    traceability.
  • VENDORS — the vendor's WHT rate, payment terms, and currency are pulled
    from their vendor profile automatically.
  • FINANCE (GL) — when a payment is recorded, the system automatically
    creates a journal entry in the general ledger via the outbox system.
  • COST CENTERS / ANALYTIC ACCOUNTS — invoice lines can be allocated to
    specific departments or projects.

Work locations and geofencing
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A work location is a specific physical place where employees work — the main
Erbil office, a construction site, a factory building. Each location has:
  • GPS coordinates (latitude and longitude)
  • A geofence radius (default 200 metres)

A geofence is an invisible circular boundary around the location. When an
employee clocks in or out using the mobile app, the system checks whether they
are physically within this circle. If they are within the radius, the punch is
marked as geofence-valid. If they are outside it, the punch is still recorded
but flagged — the HR admin can see the exact distance from the boundary.

This prevents employees from clocking in from home or another location.


Shift configurations
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A shift defines the working hours for a group of employees:
  • **Start time** — when the shift begins (e.g., 08:00)
  • **End time** — when the shift ends (e.g., 17:00)
  • **Break minutes** — lunch or prayer break time subtracted from worked hours
  • **Overtime threshold hours** — any time worked beyond this number per day
    is considered overtime (e.g., 8 hours means anything after 9 hours is OT)

Shifts are assigned to individual employees with an effective date. If an
employee switches shifts, create a new assignment with the new shift and the
date it starts from.


Bank details and why they are masked
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Each employee can have their bank details stored in the system for payroll
payment processing. Bank account numbers are masked on screen by default —
you will see asterisks instead of the full number. This protects against
accidental exposure.

To view the full bank account number:
  1. Open the employee record.
  2. Go to the BANK DETAILS section.
  3. Click REVEAL. This action is permanently logged in the audit trail,
     recording who revealed the number and when.


Salary configuration
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Each employee's pay structure is set in their salary configuration:
  • **Base salary** — the fixed monthly amount before adjustments
  • **Allowances** — additional payments (housing allowance, transport allowance,
    meal allowance). These are named items with individual amounts.
  • **Deductions** — regular deductions (loan repayments, voluntary savings).
    These reduce net pay.
  • **Effective from date** — when this salary structure takes effect.
    Previous configurations are preserved for historical payroll accuracy.


Step by step — creating an employee record
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1. Go to HR → EMPLOYEES.
  2. Click NEW EMPLOYEE.
  3. Fill in the required fields: **Employee number**, **First name**,
     **Last name**, **Job title**, **Department**, **Employment type**,
     **Hire date**.

SECTION 10 — ATTENDANCE
================================================================================

What this module is for
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The Attendance module records when employees arrive at and leave from work.
It uses GPS location checks to verify that employees are physically at their
work location when they clock in. This data feeds directly into payroll (to
calculate paid working days) and overtime tracking.


Who uses this module
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • Employees — clock in and out via the mobile app
  • HR Admin — monitors attendance, investigates anomalies, views daily records
  • Company Admin — views company-wide attendance reports
  • Payroll team — uses attendance summaries for payroll processing
  • System Admin — full access


What a punch is
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A punch is a single clock-in or clock-out event. Every working day should
have exactly two punches: one "in" punch when the employee arrives, and one
"out" punch when they leave. The system records:
  • The exact time of the punch (to the second)
  • The GPS coordinates of the employee's phone at the time of the punch
  • The distance in metres from the work location
  • Whether the punch was within the geofence (valid) or outside it (flagged)


What makes a punch valid vs flagged
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A punch is marked geofence-valid when the employee's GPS location at the time
of the punch was within the configured radius of their assigned work location
(typically 200 metres).

A punch is marked invalid when:
  • The employee was further than the radius from the work location
  • No GPS data was provided (the employee had location services turned off)

Flagged punches are still recorded — the system does not block them. But the
HR admin can see the distance and decide whether to accept or query the punch.


The attendance calendar — reading the colored dots
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The attendance calendar gives a visual overview of an employee's attendance
over a month. Each day has a colored dot:

  • Green — the employee was present and their punches were valid
  • Amber — the employee was present and worked overtime
  • Red — the employee was absent on a working day (no punches recorded)
  • Blue — the employee was on approved leave on this day
  • Grey — this was a weekend or non-working day for this employee

Click any day on the calendar to see the full punch detail for that day —
exact clock-in time, clock-out time, GPS coordinates, and distance from the
work location.


What happens if an employee forgets to clock out
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
If an employee clocks in but does not clock out, the system records a single
"in" punch with no paired "out" punch. The day is flagged as incomplete. The
HR admin can view these flagged days and manually add the missing clock-out time
after confirming with the employee or their manager. An incomplete punch does
not block payroll — the HR team handles it during the monthly payroll preparation.


How to view a specific employee's attendance
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
EMPLOYEE VIEW (own attendance only)
  1. Go to ATTENDANCE in the left sidebar.
  2. Your own attendance calendar appears by default.
  3. Use the month selector to navigate to previous months.
  4. Click any day to see punch details.

ADMIN VIEW (all employees)
  1. Go to ATTENDANCE → ALL EMPLOYEES (only visible to HR Admin and above).
  2. Use the **Employee** filter to select a specific person.
  3. Use the **From date** and **To date** filters to set the date range.
  4. Click SEARCH.
  5. The list shows every punch record for the selected employee in the period.

What this module is for

The Payroll module calculates, reviews, approves, and posts the monthly salary

Who uses this module

  • HR Admin — creates and processes payroll runs, reviews payslips
  • Company Admin — approves the payroll run before it is posted
  • Finance team — reviews the GL impact after posting

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

------------|------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------
draft       | Run created, calculations not yet started      | HR Admin creates the run      | HR Admin clicks PROCESS
processing  | System is calculating salaries                 | HR Admin clicks PROCESS       | Wait for calculations to complete
review      | Calculations done, ready for human review      | Processing completes          | HR Admin reviews, then submits

Step by step — running payroll
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1. Go to PAYROLL → PAYROLL RUNS.
  2. Click NEW PAYROLL RUN.
  3. Select the **Period start** and **Period end** (typically the first and
     last day of the month).
  4. Click CREATE. The system creates a draft run including all active employees.
  5. Click PROCESS. The system calculates each employee's pay:
       • Base salary from their salary configuration
       • Allowances from their salary configuration
       • Deductions for unpaid leave days
       • Income tax deduction (if configured for the company)
       • Social security deductions (if configured)
       • Net pay = gross - all deductions
  6. Status moves to 'review'. Open the run to see each employee's payslip.
  7. Review each payslip. Check that the gross pay, deductions, and net pay
     look correct. If something is wrong (e.g., a salary change was not entered
     in HR), correct the underlying data in HR, then re-run processing.
  8. Click SUBMIT FOR APPROVAL. The company admin is notified.
  9. The company admin opens the run, reviews the total figures, and clicks
     APPROVE. Or, if there is an issue, clicks REJECT with a reason.
  10. After approval, click POST. This is irreversible.

WHAT POSTING DOES
When you post a payroll run:
  • Every employee's payslip is locked — no further changes can be made.
  • The system automatically creates journal entries in the general ledger:
      Debit: Salary Expense account (and individual allowance accounts)
      Credit: Payroll Payable account (showing FNC now owes salaries)
  • The FX rates at the moment of processing are locked into the payslips.
    This ensures that a salary paid in USD is converted to IQD at the rate
    that was valid on the processing date, not a later rate.


Viewing and downloading payslips
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1. Go to PAYROLL → PAYSLIPS.
  2. To see all employees: use the employee filter and date range filters.
  3. To see your own: go to MY PAYSLIPS.
  4. Click any payslip row to open the full breakdown.
  5. The payslip shows:
       • Employee details and employment period
       • Attendance summary (working days, leave days, overtime hours)
       • Gross pay breakdown (base + allowances)
       • All deductions (tax, social security, other)
       • Net pay
  6. Click DOWNLOAD PDF to save a copy.


FX rates and payroll
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
If any employees are paid in a currency other than IQD (for example, an expat
paid in USD), the system converts their salary to IQD at the FX rate in effect
when the payroll run is processed. This rate is locked into the payslip record.
Even if the USD/IQD rate changes the next day, that payslip always shows the
historical rate that was used. This is critical for audit accuracy.

SECTION 12 — MANUFACTURING

What this module is for
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The Manufacturing module manages the production process at Nishtimani Factory.
It tracks what materials go into making a product, what work centres are
involved, how much it costs to produce, and how actual production compares to
the planned budget. It connects directly to inventory (consuming components
and adding finished goods) and to project costing (manufacturing costs feed

Who uses this module

  • Project Manager — tracks MOs linked to their project
  • System Admin — full access


Key concepts — Bills of Materials and Work Centers
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT A BILL OF MATERIALS (BOM) IS
A Bill of Materials (BOM) is a recipe. It lists exactly what raw materials and
components are needed to produce one unit of a finished product.

Example: To produce one prefabricated wall panel:
  • 50 kg of structural steel
  • 20 kg of lightweight concrete mix
  • 4 anchor bolts (grade 8.8)
  • 2 hours of welding labour at Work Center A

The BOM is created once and reused every time that product is manufactured.
When a manufacturing order is created from a BOM, the system automatically
populates the required materials based on the BOM.

WHAT A WORK CENTER IS
A work center is a specific machine, team, or workspace that performs a
manufacturing operation. Examples: Welding Bay 1, CNC Machine 3, Assembly Line B.
Each work center has a capacity (how many units per hour it can process).
Work centers help track where time and labour costs are being spent.


Manufacturing order (MO) statuses
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Status      | What it means                              | How you get here                  | What happens next
------------|--------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------
draft       | Order created, materials not yet reserved  | Production Manager creates MO     | Add lines, then submit
submitted   | Ready for management approval              | Production Manager submits        | Manager or admin approves
approved    | Management signed off, ready to start      | Manager clicks APPROVE            | Production Manager starts
in_progress | Production is actively under way           | Production Manager clicks START   | Record actual quantities
completed   | Production finished, goods moved to stock  | Production Manager completes      | Finished goods added to inventory
rejected    | Approval denied                            | Manager clicks REJECT             | Production Manager revises and resubmits
cancelled   | MO abandoned before completion             | Any authorised user cancels       | No stock movement


Step by step — running a manufacturing order
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 1 — CREATING THE BOM (one time per product)
  1. Go to MANUFACTURING → BILLS OF MATERIALS.
  2. Click NEW BOM.
  3. Enter the **BOM number** and select the **Finished product** (the item
     being produced).
  4. Click SAVE to create the BOM header.
  5. Click ADD COMPONENT. For each raw material needed, select the **Product**,
     enter the **Quantity per unit** (how much is needed per finished unit),
     and the **Unit cost**.
  6. Repeat for all components.
  7. Set the BOM status to 'active' when it is ready to use.

STEP 2 — CREATING A MANUFACTURING ORDER
  8. Go to MANUFACTURING → MANUFACTURING ORDERS.
  9. Click NEW ORDER.
  10. Fill in:
        • **Project** — if this production is for a specific project
        • **Description** — what is being made and why
        • **Planned start date** and **Planned end date**
        • **Quantity to produce** — how many units you intend to make
  11. If this product has an active BOM, the system offers to populate the
      component lines automatically from the BOM. Click YES to use the BOM.
  12. Review and adjust the component lines if needed.
  13. Click SAVE. Status is 'draft'.

STEP 3 — SUBMITTING AND APPROVING
  14. Click SUBMIT. Status moves to 'submitted'.
  15. A production manager or system admin reviews the order.
  16. Click APPROVE. Status moves to 'approved'.

STEP 4 — STARTING PRODUCTION
  17. Click START ORDER. Status moves to 'in_progress'.
  18. The actual start date is recorded.

STEP 5 — COMPLETING THE ORDER
  19. When production is done, click COMPLETE ORDER.
  20. Enter the **Actual quantity produced** and the **Actual component
      quantities used** for each material (these may differ from the planned
      quantities if there was waste or efficiency improvement).
  21. Click CONFIRM.
  22. The system automatically creates:
        • A stock move consuming the actual component quantities from inventory
        • A stock move adding the finished goods to the warehouse

WHAT VARIANCE MEANS
The difference between planned and actual component consumption is called
variance. If you planned to use 50 kg of steel but actually used 55 kg, you
have a 5 kg unfavourable materials variance. This is tracked in the cost analysis
tab and feeds into project profitability.


Connections to other modules
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • INVENTORY — components are reserved from stock when the MO is approved and
    consumed when it is completed. Finished goods are added to stock.
  • PROJECTS — MO costs are attributed to the linked project's cost summary.
  • FINANCE — MO cost data feeds into project invoice calculations (cost-plus
    billing method uses actual MO costs).
  • BOMS — reusable BOM templates drive the component lines on each order.

Understanding consolidated reports
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A consolidated report combines the financial data from all three FNC entities
into one set of numbers, as if they were a single company.

WHY INTER-COMPANY ELIMINATIONS MATTER
When one FNC entity buys from another FNC entity, that transaction appears as
revenue in one company and a cost in the other. If you simply added both
companies' numbers together without adjustment, you would be double-counting
money that never left the FNC Group.

Example: Nishtimani Factory provides steel fabrication services to Nishtimani
Yakam for IQD 50,000,000. In Factory's books: IQD 50M revenue. In Yakam's
books: IQD 50M expense. If you add the two P&L statements together, the group
appears to have made IQD 50M of revenue and IQD 50M of cost — which nets to
zero, which is correct. But if you accidentally only show the revenue without
the elimination, the group looks IQD 50M more profitable than it really is.

Consolidated reports with the "include eliminations" option turned on remove
all these inter-company transactions automatically, showing only the group's
true external revenues and costs.


The standard financial reports
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
TRIAL BALANCE

  balances as of a selected date. Used by the Finance team to verify that

or quarterly per regulatory requirements.

FX EXPOSURE REPORT — Shows FNC's net position in each foreign currency
(USD, EUR, etc.) — how much receivable vs payable in each currency. A large
net exposure in a foreign currency means FNC is at risk from exchange rate
movements. Finance and management should review this monthly.

PAYROLL TAX REPORT — Shows each employee's gross salary, income tax withheld,
and social security contributions. Used for filing payroll tax returns.


How to export a report
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Every report in the Reporting module has an EXPORT button at the top right of
the results table. Click it to download the data as a CSV file that can be
opened in Microsoft Excel.

SECTION 15 — INTER-COMPANY TRANSACTIONS
================================================================================

What this module is for
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
When one FNC entity provides goods, services, equipment, or funds to another
FNC entity, this must be formally recorded in both companies' accounting systems.
This is not optional — it is required for the financials of each entity to be
accurate and for the consolidated group accounts to show the correct net position.
The Inter-Company (interco) module manages these cross-entity transactions from
creation through dual approval to automatic GL posting.


Who uses this module
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • Finance team — creates interco transactions, reviews pricing
  • Company Admin — approves interco transactions (both entities must approve)
  • System Admin — can approve for both sides and post directly
  • Management — views interco balances in consolidated reports


What an inter-company transaction is
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
An inter-company transaction is any financial event between two of FNC's three
entities. Examples:
  • Nishtimani Factory transfers steel bars to a Yakam project site
  • Al Watanyia rents a crane to Nishtimani Yakam for a project
  • Nishtimani Factory charges a management fee to Al Watanyia
  • Yakam transfers petty cash to Watanyia

Each of these must be recorded so that Factory's books show revenue from the
sale, and Yakam's books show a cost from the purchase. The interco module
handles this automatically once both sides approve.


Transaction types
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • Goods transfer — physical inventory moving from one entity to another
  • Service charge — one entity invoicing another for services rendered
  • Equipment rental — one entity renting equipment to another
  • Management fee — a central entity charging group management overhead costs


Transfer pricing — why it matters
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Transfer pricing is the price at which one FNC entity sells goods or services to
another. This price matters for two reasons: it determines how profit is split
between the entities, and it must be arm's-length (resembling a real market
price) to comply with tax law.

FNC uses one of four pricing methods for inter-company transfers (configured per
entity pair in Company Settings):

  • AVCO — the transfer is priced at the inventory's current weighted average
    cost. No markup. Used when the transfer is pure cost pass-through.

  • Cost Plus — the transfer is priced at AVCO cost multiplied by (1 + markup
    percentage). Example: if AVCO cost is IQD 50,000 and the markup is 10%,
    the transfer price is IQD 55,000. The selling entity earns IQD 5,000 profit.

  • Market Price — the transfer is priced at the current market price for that
    item. The Procurement Officer must input the market price at the time of
    the transfer. This is the most accurate but requires manual input.

  • Standard Cost — the transfer is priced at the product's standard cost value
    set in the Inventory module. A fixed, predictable price.


Interco transaction statuses
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Status   | What it means                                     | How you get here                     | What happens next
---------|---------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|-------------------------------
pending  | Transaction created, awaiting approval            | Finance creates the transaction       | Both company admins must approve
approved | Both entities have signed off                     | Second company admin approves         | Finance or Sys Admin posts
executed | GL entries created in both companies              | Finance or Sys Admin clicks POST      | Transaction is final
rejected | One company rejected the transaction              | Either company admin rejects          | Finance revises and resubmits


The dual approval requirement
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Every inter-company transaction requires approval from both the sending entity
and the receiving entity. This prevents one entity from unilaterally recording
a transaction that affects another entity's books without that entity's knowledge.

Example: Watanyia charges Yakam IQD 10,000,000 for equipment rental.
  1. Watanyia's Finance team creates the interco transaction.
  2. Watanyia's Company Admin reviews and approves (this is the sending side).
  3. Yakam's Company Admin reviews and approves (this is the receiving side).
  4. Only after both approvals can the transaction be posted.

A System Admin can approve for both sides. This is useful for straightforward
routine transactions.


What posting an interco transaction does
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
When a transaction is posted, the system simultaneously creates journal entries
in BOTH companies' general ledgers. For a goods transfer:

  In the SENDING company (e.g., Factory):
    Debit: Interco Receivable from Yakam
    Credit: Inventory (reducing Factory's stock)

  In the RECEIVING company (e.g., Yakam):
    Debit: Inventory (adding Yakam's stock)
    Credit: Interco Payable to Factory

Both entries are created in a single atomic operation — either both succeed
or neither does. This ensures the two companies' books are always in sync.

For stock transfers specifically, the inventory system detects the cross-entity
move automatically and triggers the interco workflow without requiring the
Finance team to create the transaction manually.

SECTION 16 — ADMIN: USER AND COMPANY MANAGEMENT
================================================================================

What this module is for
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The Admin module is used by System Admins and Company Admins to manage who can
access the FNC ERP and what they are allowed to do. It also manages the company
entities themselves. This section covers inviting users, assigning roles,
managing companies, and monitoring the background processing queue.


Who uses this module
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • System Admin — creates companies, manages users across all entities,
    monitors system health
  • Company Admin — invites and manages users within their own entity


The four system roles — what each can do
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SYSTEM ADMIN
The most powerful role. A System Admin can see and do everything across all
three FNC entities. They can create companies, delete users, override any
workflow, approve vendor invoices, post interco transactions, and access all
financial data. Only a small number of trusted individuals should hold this role.

COMPANY ADMIN
Full access within a single company. A Company Admin can manage all users in
their company, approve projects, approve payroll runs, and view all financial
reports for their entity. They cannot access other companies' data.

MODULE ADMIN
Access to a specific set of modules within one company. For example, a Finance
Module Admin can do everything in the Finance module but has no access to HR.
When assigning this role, both the company and the specific module must be
selected. Modules available for scoping: Finance, Procurement, Inventory, HR,
Payroll, Projects, Manufacturing, Rental, Reporting, Inter-Company, Admin.

USER
Standard access. A User can perform day-to-day tasks in the modules they have
been given access to, but cannot manage other users, approve payroll runs,
or access admin functions.


How to invite a new user
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1. Go to ADMIN → USERS.
  2. Click INVITE USER.
  3. Enter the new user's **Email address**.
  4. Select their **Role** and **Company**.
  5. If Module Admin: select the specific **Module**.
  6. Click SEND INVITATION.
  7. The user receives an email with a link to set up their account and
     choose their password.
  8. The invitation link expires after 7 days. If the user misses the
     deadline, send a new invitation.


How to create a user directly (without an invitation email)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1. Go to ADMIN → USERS.
  2. Click NEW USER.
  3. Enter the **Email address** and a **Password** (minimum 8 characters).
  4. Assign the **Role** and **Company**.
  5. Click CREATE. The user account is active immediately.
  6. Communicate the credentials to the user securely — the system does not
     send a welcome email in this flow.


How to unlock a locked account
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
If a user has been locked out after too many failed login attempts:
  1. Go to ADMIN → USERS.
  2. Search for the user by email.
  3. Click the user row to open their profile.
  4. Click UNLOCK ACCOUNT.
  5. The failed attempt counter resets and the user can log in again.


How to reset MFA for a user
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
If a user has lost access to their authenticator app:
  1. Go to ADMIN → USERS.
  2. Open the user's profile.
  3. Click RESET MFA.
  4. The user's MFA is disabled. All their sessions are terminated.
  5. The user must log in with their password and set up MFA again from
     their Settings page.


Creating a new company
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1. Go to ADMIN → COMPANIES.
  2. Click NEW COMPANY.
  3. Fill in all company details:
       • Legal name and trading name
       • Country, city, address
       • Registration number and VAT number
       • Bank details
       • Inter-company transfer pricing method
  4. Click CREATE.
  5. The system automatically creates for the new company:
       • 12 monthly accounting periods for the current year
       • 3 default stock locations (Main Warehouse, Virtual In, Virtual Out)
       • A default system configuration record


The outbox monitor — what it is and when to check it
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Many FNC ERP actions trigger background tasks — sending notification emails,
creating GL journal entries, generating PDFs, sending approval alerts. These
tasks are placed in a queue called the outbox and processed automatically by
background workers.

Go to ADMIN → OUTBOX MONITOR to see the status of these background tasks.

Under normal operation, the outbox should be empty or contain only recent
items being processed. If you see many items stuck in 'pending' status for
more than a few minutes, something may be wrong with the background workers.
Contact the system administrator.


The DLQ — dead letter queue
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Go to ADMIN → DLQ.

If an outbox task fails multiple times (the system retries automatically),
it is moved to the DLQ — the dead letter queue. Items in the DLQ are events
that the system tried to process but could not complete.

CRITICAL: If items appear in the DLQ, check them immediately. They may
represent journal entries that did not post, payments not recorded, or
notifications not sent. Each item shows the event type and the error message.
Common items that are serious:
  • VENDOR_PAYMENT_JOURNAL_REQUESTED — a vendor payment was recorded but
    the GL entry was not created. The Finance team must manually post the entry.
  • PAYROLL_FINALIZED — a payroll run was posted but the salary expense journal
    was not created.
  • PROJECT_INVOICE_ISSUED — an invoice was issued but the AR entry was not
    created.

Review the DLQ every day. Do not let items accumulate.


System health
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Go to ADMIN → SYSTEM HEALTH.

This page shows 12 service status cards covering every backend component of
the FNC ERP. Each card shows:
  • GREEN — the service is running normally
  • AMBER — the service is running but with warnings (e.g., slow response)
  • RED — the service is down or returning errors

If any card is red, contact the system administrator immediately. A red finance
service means journal entries cannot be posted. A red auth service means users
cannot log in. A red inventory service means stock receipts are not being
processed.

SECTION 17 — SETTINGS AND PREFERENCES
================================================================================

What this module is for
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The Settings module allows each user to personalise their experience of the
FNC ERP and manage their account security. Company Admins also use this module
to configure company-wide settings like tax rates, default currencies, and FX
alert thresholds.


Personal security settings
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CHANGING YOUR PASSWORD
  1. Go to SETTINGS → SECURITY.
  2. Click CHANGE PASSWORD.
  3. Enter your **Current password**.
  4. Enter your **New password** (minimum 8 characters).
  5. Enter the new password again in **Confirm new password**.
  6. Click SAVE.
  7. All your active sessions are terminated and you will need to log in again
     with the new password.

SETTING UP MFA (DETAILED STEPS)
  1. Go to SETTINGS → SECURITY.
  2. Click ENABLE TWO-FACTOR AUTHENTICATION.
  3. A QR code appears on screen.
  4. On your phone, open Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator.
  5. Tap the + button in the app, then select SCAN QR CODE.
  6. Point your camera at the QR code on screen.
  7. The app adds FNC ERP to its list and starts showing a six-digit code
     that changes every 30 seconds.
  8. Back in the browser, enter the current six-digit code in the
     **Verification code** field.
  9. Click CONFIRM. MFA is now active.
  10. From this point on, you will need to enter a code from the app every
      time you log in.

VIEWING AND REVOKING YOUR ACTIVE SESSIONS
  1. Go to SETTINGS → SECURITY → ACTIVE SESSIONS.
  2. You will see a list of all devices currently logged in with your account.
     Each entry shows the device name, operating system, IP address, and when
     the session was created.
  3. If you see a session you do not recognise (e.g., a device you do not own),
     click REVOKE next to that session to log it out immediately.
  4. To log out of all devices everywhere: click REVOKE ALL SESSIONS.


Display preferences
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CHANGING YOUR THEME
  1. Go to SETTINGS → APPEARANCE.
  2. Click one of the four theme options:
       • Dark Glass — preferred for offices with screens in lower light
       • Dark White — clean dark mode
       • Light Glass — glass effect for bright environments
       • Light Flat — simplest light theme, lowest visual weight
  3. The theme changes immediately without needing to save.

DATE AND NUMBER FORMAT
  1. Go to SETTINGS → APPEARANCE.
  2. Under **Date format**, select your preference:
       • DD/MM/YYYY — standard Iraqi/European format (e.g., 15/06/2025)
       • MM/DD/YYYY — US format (e.g., 06/15/2025)
       • YYYY-MM-DD — international ISO format (e.g., 2025-06-15)
  3. Under **Number format**, select:
       • Standard — 1,234,567.89 (commas as thousands separator)
       • European — 1.234.567,89 (dots as thousands separator)
  4. Click SAVE.

NOTIFICATION PREFERENCES
  1. Go to SETTINGS → NOTIFICATIONS.
  2. You will see a list of notification types. For each type you can toggle:
       • Email — receive an email for this event
       • In-app — see a notification in the bell icon in the top bar
  3. Common notification types and what they mean:
       • PO approval required — someone needs your approval on a PO
       • PO approved / rejected — action was taken on a PO you submitted
       • Leave request submitted — an employee under you submitted leave
       • Leave approved / rejected — action was taken on your leave request
       • Vendor invoice submitted — a vendor invoice needs your approval
       • Payroll run submitted — a payroll run needs your approval
       • New device login — a new device logged into your account
  4. Click SAVE.


Company settings (Company Admin only)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1. Go to SETTINGS → COMPANY SETTINGS.
  2. This section allows the Company Admin to configure:
       • **Fiscal year** — start month and day of the company's accounting year
       • **Default currency** — the primary currency for this entity (IQD)
       • **Default PO currency** — the currency purchase orders default to
       • **Default payment terms** — standard days for vendor invoices
       • **Income tax** — enable/disable and set the income tax rate
       • **Social security rates** — employee and employer contribution percentages
       • **WHT rates** — default withholding tax rates by category
       • **Email settings** — the reply-to email for system notifications
  3. Click SAVE CONFIGURATION after making changes.

FX ALERT THRESHOLDS
  1. Go to SETTINGS → COMPANY SETTINGS.
  2. Scroll to the FX Rates section.
  3. Set the **Warning threshold** — how many hours without an FX rate update
     triggers an amber warning on the Finance FX page.
  4. Set the **Critical threshold** — how many hours triggers a red alert and
     blocks new journal entries without an override.
  5. Recommended values for IQD:
       • Warning: 24 hours (rates should update daily)
       • Critical: 48 hours (anything older than 2 days is unreliable)
  6. Click SAVE.
