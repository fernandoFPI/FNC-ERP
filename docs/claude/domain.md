# Domain decisions (settled, don't relitigate)

- `products.valuation_method` is permanently `last_cost` (migration 257 dropped avco/fifo/standard).
  Weighted-average costing is not coming back.
- Stock availability is scoped company-wide on purpose (G10 cancelled). Don't propose branch scoping.
- Purchasing: `purchase_orders` is split into `requisitions` (pre-vendor) + per-vendor child POs created
  at Finish Buying. No currency conversion; each line keeps its own `currency_code`.
- Vendor is never required at PO creation. Actual price is entered at the `items_bought` stage; Finance
  Audit is a read-only review.
- GraphQL gateway is the canonical API for the PO workflow (the old REST procurement service was removed).
- Variation Orders: approval syncs contract value + cost budget via `applied_value` bookkeeping
  (reversible); approved = locked snapshot (voValue/contractId not editable). Approval logs an entry in
  `project_contract_revisions`.
- Contracts and Bids use read-only `RevisionHistory`; Eng Docs/Client Docs keep their own actionable
  revisioning (don't force the read-only component onto them).
- Project lifecycle phases, tab labels and which phase a tab appears from are config-driven
  (Settings > Company > Project Lifecycle). Tab visibility/edit/approve come from the capability
  resolver (`lib/projectCapabilityMatrix.ts`, `hooks/useProjectCapability.ts`).
- Client-requested tab names are intentional (see CLAUDE.md).
- Inventory integrity: a negative-balance trigger guard exists (migration 254); opening-balance backfill
  done (255/256). Landed cost and a cost-only revaluation move are open follow-ups.
- Cost Change Approvals (`product_cost_change_requests`, Inventory > Cost Approvals): whoever triggered a parked
  cost change can't be the one to clear it, except admins (system_admin/company_admin) and the company-wide
  Store Keeper position (a Store Keeper scoped to a branch, department or project does not count). One function,
  `canReviewOwnCostChangeGW` (gateway resolvers), answers it for approve, reject and the queue's per-row
  `can_review`; the web page keeps no copy of the rule. A self-reviewed row is labelled "Reviewed by the person
  who raised it". Rejecting holds back only the product's own cost: the receipt, price, adjustment or edit that
  raised the request stays saved (stock adjustments and manual edits keep the stock location's cost).
- Stock-covered lines and the delivery destination: a project requisition/PO's delivery destination only decides
  what happens to PURCHASED goods (Delivered to inventory: a Store In; Delivered to the jobsite: straight to the
  jobsite, no Store In). Whatever is covered from inventory ALWAYS gets a draft Store Out (never skipped because of
  the destination), which reserves its quantity until it is confirmed; confirming deducts the stock, releases the
  reservation and charges the project. A requisition completes only when those Store Outs are confirmed. One
  function, `coverRequisitionStockLines` (gateway resolvers), makes the drafts at approval and when a buyer resolves
  a line from stock at Items Bought. The destination is chosen at creation, long before approval.
- One inventory for the whole group, held in Nishtimani Factory (companies.is_central_warehouse). Companies used to keep their own stock; now Yakam and Al Watanyia draw from Factory through cross-company Store Outs. Moving leftover stock into Factory is a no-charge re-assignment (no bill, no price, no journal), never an inter-company sale. Never match items across companies by SKU: shared SKUs are different items.
- Invoice header block (migration 311): the top of a project invoice reads Date of Issue / Due Date / Invoice No., then Deliver To and Bill To boxes, then Contract No. / Job / Period of Performance / Date of Delivery / Reference. Contract No. is the CLIENT's number (invoice `client_contract_no`, prefilled from `projects.rfq_number`), never our internal `project_contracts.contract_number`; Reference is our project code, Job the project name. Bill To / Deliver To / Date of Delivery / Period are free text kept on the invoice, prefilled from the project and editable at creation and on the edit screen; NULL = derive from the project, '' = blank on purpose.
- Expense claims sent back: a rejected claim stays `rejected` and is editable by whoever filed it (or an admin), then resubmitted (`POST /finance/expense-claims/:id/revise`); a claim Finance sent back from audit (`draft`) is editable the same way. Lines carry a stable number (`line_number`, never reused or shifted) and an approver may flag individual lines with a reason when rejecting, but flags are optional (a whole-claim reason is enough, unlike requisitions). The reject panel asks for one reason at a time: the claim-level one while no line is ticked, each ticked line's own reason once one is (the server builds the claim reason from them). The editor and the server decide who may edit in one place (`canReviseClaim`); finance.expenses.edit holders can edit drafts, not other people's rejected claims.
- Invoices in review can be rejected (migration 313): an approver (same gate as Approve, `projects.invoices.edit`) sends a project invoice from `review` back to `draft` with optional flags on lines and on the ten fields of the printed header (Date of Issue, Due Date, Invoice No., Deliver To, Bill To, Contract No., Job, Period of Performance, Date of Delivery, Reference) and a reason, one reason field at a time (the invoice-level one while nothing is ticked, each ticked item's own once one is). The creator fixes it in draft and uses Submit for review again, which marks the flags addressed; the approver confirms each flagged line or header field with Confirm fixed. The flags and the rejection are internal: never on the public QR verification page or the printed invoice. Nothing is posted before Approve, so a rejection undoes nothing.
- Organizer-handled lines (migration 315): at Market pricing the requisition's organizer may take the lines he will price and buy himself (`po_lines.owner_handles`, only while a line has no market price; procurement and admins can hand any line back). Procurement prices and buys the rest. Market pricing is submitted in two parts (organizer's lines, procurement's lines); the requisition moves to Price verification once each part that has lines has been submitted, and a side with no lines counts as done, so a requisition where the organizer took nothing behaves as before. The organizer may record purchases, mark short and resolve from stock only on his own lines; procurement can step in on any line, including submitting the organizer's part. Finish Buying happens by itself once every purchased line is bought or marked short, every purchase has a receipt and every over-tolerance purchase is approved (the button stays as the fallback). The organizer never gets the Finish Buying button, and still cannot approve an over-tolerance purchase. Each line shows who handles it (Organizer or Procurement; a dash when stock covers it) in the Lines table from Market pricing on, and on the Items Bought page, with a name beside it: whoever recorded a purchase on the line, else the organizer on his lines, else the procurement officer who priced the requisition (procurement has no named person per line before a purchase).
- Extra approval on Items Bought (migration 316): an admin (system_admin, or the PO Admin position, like PO positions) can mark a user, per company, in Settings > the user's Roles tab, so that purchases that user records need the requisition organizer's approval. It works for a procurement position holder and a normal user alike. The purchase remembers whether its buyer was marked when it was recorded, so marking or un-marking later changes nothing already recorded. A requisition with such a purchase stays in Items Bought after its last purchase (Finish Buying has a fourth gate) until the organizer, or an admin acting for him, approves; the organizer is notified and sees it in his queue. Approving is one button for the whole requisition; any single waiting purchase can instead be sent back with a reason (it is removed and the buyer records it again and is told why). Nobody ever approves or sends back a purchase they bought themselves, so when the marked user is the organizer (a normal user buying his own lines) only an admin can. An unmarked buyer's purchases are unaffected, and the over-tolerance sign-off is a separate gate that still applies.
- Bank account on an invoice: a wire-transfer project invoice must have an ACTIVE bank account chosen (`project_invoices.bank_account_id`) before it can be submitted for review, approved or issued; a Cash invoice needs none. Without one the printed invoice and its preview carry no Banking Information block. The server refuses with `BANK_ACCOUNT_REQUIRED` and the invoice page shows a red warning with the three buttons held back. Reading the bank list needs `finance.ar.view`; someone without it cannot see bank details on any invoice until granted. A paid or issued invoice cannot be edited from the app, so a missing account on one is fixed by a one-row SQL update after a backup, with an audit row.
- Letterhead invoice furniture: the title block top right carries only INVOICE (no number, the Invoice No. box holds it; no QR code, letterhead artwork often fills that corner). Every page shows the verification QR code bottom left and "Page N of M" bottom right, both in the band above the letterhead's footer artwork (level with the stamp), with "Continued on next page →" under the page number on every page but the last. Later pages show the invoice number on the "Continued from previous page" row, since they have no Invoice No. box. There is no company name / invoice number footer and no rule. The plain (no letterhead) layout is unchanged (its QR code stays in the bottom row).
- Vendor returns and project cost (migration 317): an approved or credited vendor return takes its value off the project cost of a jobsite delivery (a negative `po_vendor_return` cost row per return item, in the return's own currency) and off what the PO pays (the completion journal posts ordered minus returned, per currency; a fully returned currency posts nothing). It happens at APPROVAL and crediting adds nothing; Claude chose approval on 2026-11-07 without the user's answer, so confirm it. The PO keeps its lines and ordered totals; the PO page shows Returned and After returns per currency, computed by the server. A project PO delivered to inventory costs the project nothing until a Store Out, so returning it takes nothing off the project. Jobsite delivery cost is booked in the LINE's currency (never a hard-coded IQD), each delivery is added at the price current when it arrives, and an approved price edit or admin correction re-prices what was delivered while a removed line drops its cost; `lib/directDeliveryCost.ts` is the only place that writes it.
- Ownership of a forked PO: a child purchase order created at Finish Buying continues the requisition's ownership. Its `created_by` and `organizer_id` are both the requisition's organizer, never the buyer or admin whose action happened to finish the buying (that person is in `requisition_approval_log`, action `finish_buying`). Only a requisition with no organizer falls back to the finisher, because `created_by` is NOT NULL. POs forked before this were corrected by `scripts/backfill-po-ownership.ts` (old value kept in `audit_log`).
- Cost and currency label on a stock location: a location's last cost (`stock_balances.average_cost` with `last_cost_currency`) is a copy taken when stock last moved or a cost correction was made; the product's own Cost (`products.standard_cost` with `cost_currency`) is the figure people correct and the one the Products list shows (never the legacy `products.average_cost`; a cost of 0 means never recorded and shows a dash). The product's currency is the trusted one, and the user has both IQD and USD products. A Stock Adjustment cost correction updates the location when the amount OR the currency differs (a location holding nothing is re-tagged directly, with no stock move). Stock tagged IQD only by the column default, under a USD product at the same amount, is relabelled USD by `scripts/backfill-location-cost-currency.ts`; rows with a different amount are listed for a person, never changed by a script. Do not sync every location to the product cost whenever it changes: that would revalue stock outside the ledger. The Stock Balances page shows every row in its own currency and one Total Inventory Value per currency (`stockBalanceSnapshot.totals`, summed in SQL), never one number across currencies.
- Unit of measure on an invoice line (migration 318): every project invoice line can carry a unit (Ea, M, Lot, Roll, Set, Box, Kg, L, Pack, Pair, Day, or any text up to 20 characters), printed in a Unit column right after Qty and shown in the invoice page's lines table. The creation wizard and the edit screen offer the list or a typed unit; a new line starts as Ea. NULL means never set: every line made before the column existed (and every line the server is asked to store without one) prints a dash, and nothing is guessed. The server has no list of its own and accepts any short text, so the list is a convenience only. A unit left out of an edit keeps the stored one; an empty one clears it. The public QR page returns the unit with the other printed fields.
- Financing phase (migration 319): the project lifecycle is now Client Enquiry, Scope Review, Bidding, Client Approval, Execution, **Financing**, Closeout. Execution ends with **End Execution** (asks first), which moves the project to Financing; the finance team then issues the project's invoices and watches them get paid on the existing Contract Management tab (no separate tab). **Close Project** moved from Execution to Financing and is only possible when every invoice is paid. Financing is a PHASE, not a status: `projects.status` stays `approved` throughout, so nothing that checks for an approved project changes, and nothing else about Execution (its tabs, POs, receipts) is locked. Who may end execution and close out: the project's own manager (`project_manager_id`) or anyone holding `projects.approve` (admins pass), one function `callerCanCloseOutProjectGW`. The invoice rule (`packages/db/src/projectCloseout.ts`, shared by the gateway and the REST `/projects/:id/complete`): an unpaid invoice (anything not `paid`, `cancelled` or `void`) blocks everyone, administrators included; a project with no invoices (or only cancelled/void ones) blocks everyone except an administrator (system/company admin or a projects module admin). Cancelled and void invoices are not invoices. The Admin Override panel still moves a project to any phase, which is the escape hatch. Financing has no Cancel Project button (Execution keeps its own). No project was moved by the migration: those in Execution stay there until someone presses End Execution, and those already in Closeout stay in Closeout. Labels are company config (Settings > Company > Project Lifecycle), so a company may rename the phase; the Arabic label is "التمويل".
- Withholding tax (WHT) on a project (migration 320): the new-RFQ and edit-project forms no longer have Budget Amount; in its place a Withholding Tax block carries the same three settings an invoice has: applies (off by default), scenario (`client_withholds` = the client deducts it and pays the tax authority, `fnc_pays` = the client pays in full and FNC remits annually) and rate (typed as a percent, default 3%, 0 to 100 with at most two decimals, stored as a fraction in `projects.wht_rate`). The system shows how much tax the project will carry = **Bid Price (the typed "Bid Price (Project Value)", `projects.project_value`) x rate**, rounded to four decimals like an invoice; the amount is never stored, it is calculated on every read, and it is empty (not zero) until a Bid Price is typed. The Bidding tab's computed bid price is not the base (unused on production). The Currency selector on the form labels the Bid Price as well as the project, so the tax carries the right currency. The project Overview shows a Withholding Tax row (amount, rate, scenario) when WHT applies. This is an estimate on the project only: invoices keep their own WHT settings, typed per invoice, and are not prefilled from the project. Budget: nothing in the UI sets a project's Approved Budget any more; a budget a project already has is kept (an edit never wipes it), the API still accepts `budgetAmount`, and on production 1 of 152 projects had one. Permissions are unchanged: the tax fields follow the permissions of the project fields beside them.
- Bidding tab file areas (migration 321): the Bidding Stage has four sub-tabs, in this order: Technical Bid, Commercial Bid, **Cost Analysis**, Clarifications Bid. Each is a plain file area where people with edit access upload files (any type, up to the 50 MB upload ceiling), download them and remove them; a ZIP can be opened and previewed. They share one list, one upload and one delete (`bidPackageFiles`, `uploadBidPackageFile`, `deleteBidPackageFile`, `lib/bidPackages.ts`), all on `projects.bidding.view` / `projects.bidding.edit`, and the page's per-member capability decides who sees the buttons. Cost Analysis and Commercial Bid are pricing material: both are hidden from a technical-only team member (admins, the PM, holders of `projects.edit` and commercial or no-membership viewers see them), on the page AND at the API (list, upload, delete; one flag per area in `lib/bidPackages.ts`). The Commercial builder queries of the full bid mode (`bidCostItems`, `bidSupplierQuotations`, `bidCommercialSummary`) still need only `projects.bidding.view`; all three companies run simple mode. Cost Analysis and Clarifications show their file area in simple and full bid mode (there is no builder behind them). Clarifications Bid used to be the Technical Queries screen (`project_tqs`, gated by `projects.qaqc.*`); it was removed on 2026-11-10 with its GraphQL operations (production held 0 TQs and 0 attachments). The empty `project_tqs` table and the `tq` entity-type value are kept so a code-only rollback still works; drop them in a later migration if wanted.
- Other Currency Budgets card (D2): costs booked in a currency other than the project's main budget currency can never be taken off that budget (no conversion anywhere), so each such currency gets its own card, with its own budget if an administrator added one ("+ Add Currency Budget"). It lives at the TOP of the project's Cost Control tab, above its sub-sections (`components/projects/OtherCurrencyBudgets.tsx`), no longer as a strip under the page header on every tab (moved 2026-11-10 at the user's request). Shown only to the page-wide administrator (`projects.edit`, the same rule as before, not the Cost Control edit right) and only while some currency has costs or the add form is open; so someone who is an administrator but cannot open the Cost Control tab no longer sees it.
