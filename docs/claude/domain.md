# Domain decisions (settled, don't relitigate)

- `products.valuation_method` is permanently `last_cost` (migration 257 dropped avco/fifo/standard).
  Weighted-average costing is not coming back.
- Stock availability is scoped company-wide on purpose (G10 cancelled). Don't propose branch scoping.
- Purchasing: `purchase_orders` is split into `requisitions` (pre-vendor) + per-vendor child POs created
  at Finish Buying. No currency conversion; each line keeps its own `currency_code`.
- Vendor is never required at PO creation. Actual price is entered at the `items_bought` stage; Finance
  Audit is a read-only review.
- GraphQL gateway is the canonical API for the PO workflow (the old REST procurement service was removed).
- Variation Orders: approval syncs contract value + cost budget via `applied_value` bookkeeping
  (reversible); approved = locked snapshot (voValue/contractId not editable). Approval logs an entry in
  `project_contract_revisions`.
- Contracts and Bids use read-only `RevisionHistory`; Eng Docs/Client Docs keep their own actionable
  revisioning (don't force the read-only component onto them).
- Project lifecycle phases, tab labels and which phase a tab appears from are config-driven
  (Settings > Company > Project Lifecycle). Tab visibility/edit/approve come from the capability
  resolver (`lib/projectCapabilityMatrix.ts`, `hooks/useProjectCapability.ts`).
- Client-requested tab names are intentional (see CLAUDE.md).
- Inventory integrity: a negative-balance trigger guard exists (migration 254); opening-balance backfill
  done (255/256). Landed cost and a cost-only revaluation move are open follow-ups.
- Cost Change Approvals (`product_cost_change_requests`, Inventory > Cost Approvals): whoever triggered a parked
  cost change can't be the one to clear it, except admins (system_admin/company_admin) and the company-wide
  Store Keeper position (a Store Keeper scoped to a branch, department or project does not count). One function,
  `canReviewOwnCostChangeGW` (gateway resolvers), answers it for approve, reject and the queue's per-row
  `can_review`; the web page keeps no copy of the rule. A self-reviewed row is labelled "Reviewed by the person
  who raised it". Rejecting holds back only the product's own cost: the receipt, price, adjustment or edit that
  raised the request stays saved (stock adjustments and manual edits keep the stock location's cost).
- Stock-covered lines and the delivery destination: a project requisition/PO's delivery destination only decides
  what happens to PURCHASED goods (Delivered to inventory: a Store In; Delivered to the jobsite: straight to the
  jobsite, no Store In). Whatever is covered from inventory ALWAYS gets a draft Store Out (never skipped because of
  the destination), which reserves its quantity until it is confirmed; confirming deducts the stock, releases the
  reservation and charges the project. A requisition completes only when those Store Outs are confirmed. One
  function, `coverRequisitionStockLines` (gateway resolvers), makes the drafts at approval and when a buyer resolves
  a line from stock at Items Bought. The destination is chosen at creation, long before approval.
- One inventory for the whole group, held in Nishtimani Factory (companies.is_central_warehouse). Companies used to keep their own stock; now Yakam and Al Watanyia draw from Factory through cross-company Store Outs. Moving leftover stock into Factory is a no-charge re-assignment (no bill, no price, no journal), never an inter-company sale. Never match items across companies by SKU: shared SKUs are different items.
- Invoice header block (migration 311): the top of a project invoice reads Date of Issue / Due Date / Invoice No., then Deliver To and Bill To boxes, then Contract No. / Job / Period of Performance / Date of Delivery / Reference. Contract No. is the CLIENT's number (invoice `client_contract_no`, prefilled from `projects.rfq_number`), never our internal `project_contracts.contract_number`; Reference is our project code, Job the project name. Bill To / Deliver To / Date of Delivery / Period are free text kept on the invoice, prefilled from the project and editable at creation and on the edit screen; NULL = derive from the project, '' = blank on purpose.
- Expense claims sent back: a rejected claim stays `rejected` and is editable by whoever filed it (or an admin), then resubmitted (`POST /finance/expense-claims/:id/revise`); a claim Finance sent back from audit (`draft`) is editable the same way. Lines carry a stable number (`line_number`, never reused or shifted) and an approver may flag individual lines with a reason when rejecting, but flags are optional (a whole-claim reason is enough, unlike requisitions). The reject panel asks for one reason at a time: the claim-level one while no line is ticked, each ticked line's own reason once one is (the server builds the claim reason from them). The editor and the server decide who may edit in one place (`canReviseClaim`); finance.expenses.edit holders can edit drafts, not other people's rejected claims.
- Invoices in review can be rejected (migration 313): an approver (same gate as Approve, `projects.invoices.edit`) sends a project invoice from `review` back to `draft` with optional flags on lines and on the ten fields of the printed header (Date of Issue, Due Date, Invoice No., Deliver To, Bill To, Contract No., Job, Period of Performance, Date of Delivery, Reference) and a reason, one reason field at a time (the invoice-level one while nothing is ticked, each ticked item's own once one is). The creator fixes it in draft and uses Submit for review again, which marks the flags addressed; the approver confirms each flagged line or header field with Confirm fixed. The flags and the rejection are internal: never on the public QR verification page or the printed invoice. Nothing is posted before Approve, so a rejection undoes nothing.
- Organizer-handled lines (migration 315): at Market pricing the requisition's organizer may take the lines he will price and buy himself (`po_lines.owner_handles`, only while a line has no market price; procurement and admins can hand any line back). Procurement prices and buys the rest. Market pricing is submitted in two parts (organizer's lines, procurement's lines); the requisition moves to Price verification once each part that has lines has been submitted, and a side with no lines counts as done, so a requisition where the organizer took nothing behaves as before. The organizer may record purchases, mark short and resolve from stock only on his own lines; procurement can step in on any line, including submitting the organizer's part. Finish Buying happens by itself once every purchased line is bought or marked short, every purchase has a receipt and every over-tolerance purchase is approved (the button stays as the fallback). The organizer never gets the Finish Buying button, and still cannot approve an over-tolerance purchase. Each line shows who handles it (Organizer or Procurement; a dash when stock covers it) in the Lines table from Market pricing on, and on the Items Bought page, with a name beside it: whoever recorded a purchase on the line, else the organizer on his lines, else the procurement officer who priced the requisition (procurement has no named person per line before a purchase).
- Extra approval on Items Bought (migration 316): an admin (system_admin, or the PO Admin position, like PO positions) can mark a user, per company, in Settings > the user's Roles tab, so that purchases that user records need the requisition organizer's approval. It works for a procurement position holder and a normal user alike. The purchase remembers whether its buyer was marked when it was recorded, so marking or un-marking later changes nothing already recorded. A requisition with such a purchase stays in Items Bought after its last purchase (Finish Buying has a fourth gate) until the organizer, or an admin acting for him, approves; the organizer is notified and sees it in his queue. Approving is one button for the whole requisition; any single waiting purchase can instead be sent back with a reason (it is removed and the buyer records it again and is told why). Nobody ever approves or sends back a purchase they bought themselves, so when the marked user is the organizer (a normal user buying his own lines) only an admin can. An unmarked buyer's purchases are unaffected, and the over-tolerance sign-off is a separate gate that still applies.
- Bank account on an invoice: a wire-transfer project invoice must have an ACTIVE bank account chosen (`project_invoices.bank_account_id`) before it can be submitted for review, approved or issued; a Cash invoice needs none. Without one the printed invoice and its preview carry no Banking Information block. The server refuses with `BANK_ACCOUNT_REQUIRED` and the invoice page shows a red warning with the three buttons held back. Reading the bank list needs `finance.ar.view`; someone without it cannot see bank details on any invoice until granted. A paid or issued invoice cannot be edited from the app, so a missing account on one is fixed by a one-row SQL update after a backup, with an audit row.
- Letterhead invoice furniture: the title block top right carries only INVOICE (no number, the Invoice No. box holds it; no QR code, letterhead artwork often fills that corner). Every page shows the verification QR code bottom left and "Page N of M" bottom right, both in the band above the letterhead's footer artwork (level with the stamp), with "Continued on next page →" under the page number on every page but the last. Later pages show the invoice number on the "Continued from previous page" row, since they have no Invoice No. box. There is no company name / invoice number footer and no rule. The plain (no letterhead) layout is unchanged (its QR code stays in the bottom row).
- Vendor returns and project cost (migration 317): an approved or credited vendor return takes its value off the project cost of a jobsite delivery (a negative `po_vendor_return` cost row per return item, in the return's own currency) and off what the PO pays (the completion journal posts ordered minus returned, per currency; a fully returned currency posts nothing). It happens at APPROVAL and crediting adds nothing; Claude chose approval on 2026-11-07 without the user's answer, so confirm it. The PO keeps its lines and ordered totals; the PO page shows Returned and After returns per currency, computed by the server. A project PO delivered to inventory costs the project nothing until a Store Out, so returning it takes nothing off the project. Jobsite delivery cost is booked in the LINE's currency (never a hard-coded IQD), each delivery is added at the price current when it arrives, and an approved price edit or admin correction re-prices what was delivered while a removed line drops its cost; `lib/directDeliveryCost.ts` is the only place that writes it.
- Ownership of a forked PO: a child purchase order created at Finish Buying continues the requisition's ownership. Its `created_by` and `organizer_id` are both the requisition's organizer, never the buyer or admin whose action happened to finish the buying (that person is in `requisition_approval_log`, action `finish_buying`). Only a requisition with no organizer falls back to the finisher, because `created_by` is NOT NULL. POs forked before this were corrected by `scripts/backfill-po-ownership.ts` (old value kept in `audit_log`).
- Cost and currency label on a stock location: a location's last cost (`stock_balances.average_cost` with `last_cost_currency`) is a copy taken when stock last moved or a cost correction was made; the product's own Cost (`products.standard_cost` with `cost_currency`) is the figure people correct and the one the Products list shows (never the legacy `products.average_cost`; a cost of 0 means never recorded and shows a dash). The product's currency is the trusted one, and the user has both IQD and USD products. A Stock Adjustment cost correction updates the location when the amount OR the currency differs (a location holding nothing is re-tagged directly, with no stock move). Stock tagged IQD only by the column default, under a USD product at the same amount, is relabelled USD by `scripts/backfill-location-cost-currency.ts`; rows with a different amount are listed for a person, never changed by a script. Do not sync every location to the product cost whenever it changes: that would revalue stock outside the ledger. The Stock Balances page shows every row in its own currency and one Total Inventory Value per currency (`stockBalanceSnapshot.totals`, summed in SQL), never one number across currencies.
- Unit of measure on an invoice line (migration 318): every project invoice line can carry a unit (Ea, M, Lot, Roll, Set, Box, Kg, L, Pack, Pair, Day, or any text up to 20 characters), printed in a Unit column right after Qty and shown in the invoice page's lines table. The creation wizard and the edit screen offer the list or a typed unit; a new line starts as Ea. NULL means never set: every line made before the column existed (and every line the server is asked to store without one) prints a dash, and nothing is guessed. The server has no list of its own and accepts any short text, so the list is a convenience only. A unit left out of an edit keeps the stored one; an empty one clears it. The public QR page returns the unit with the other printed fields.
- Financing phase (migration 319): the project lifecycle is now Client Enquiry, Scope Review, Bidding, Client Approval, Execution, **Financing**, Closeout. Execution ends with **End Execution** (asks first), which moves the project to Financing; the finance team then issues the project's invoices and watches them get paid on the existing Contract Management tab (no separate tab). **Close Project** moved from Execution to Financing and is only possible when every invoice is paid. Financing is a PHASE, not a status: `projects.status` stays `approved` throughout, so nothing that checks for an approved project changes, and nothing else about Execution (its tabs, POs, receipts) is locked. Who may end execution and close out: the project's own manager (`project_manager_id`) or anyone holding `projects.approve` (admins pass), one function `callerCanCloseOutProjectGW`. The invoice rule (`packages/db/src/projectCloseout.ts`, shared by the gateway and the REST `/projects/:id/complete`): an unpaid invoice (anything not `paid`, `cancelled` or `void`) blocks everyone, administrators included; a project with no invoices (or only cancelled/void ones) blocks everyone except an administrator (system/company admin or a projects module admin). Cancelled and void invoices are not invoices. The Admin Override panel still moves a project to any phase, which is the escape hatch. Financing has no Cancel Project button (Execution keeps its own). No project was moved by the migration: those in Execution stay there until someone presses End Execution, and those already in Closeout stay in Closeout. Labels are company config (Settings > Company > Project Lifecycle), so a company may rename the phase; the Arabic label is "التمويل".
- Withholding tax (WHT) on a project (migration 320): the new-RFQ and edit-project forms no longer have Budget Amount; in its place a Withholding Tax block carries the same three settings an invoice has: applies (off by default), scenario (`client_withholds` = the client deducts it and pays the tax authority, `fnc_pays` = the client pays in full and FNC remits annually) and rate (typed as a percent, default 3%, 0 to 100 with at most two decimals, stored as a fraction in `projects.wht_rate`). The system shows how much tax the project will carry = **Bid Price (the typed "Bid Price (Project Value)", `projects.project_value`) x rate**, rounded to four decimals like an invoice; the amount is never stored, it is calculated on every read, and it is empty (not zero) until a Bid Price is typed. The Bidding tab's computed bid price is not the base (unused on production). The Currency selector on the form labels the Bid Price as well as the project, so the tax carries the right currency. The project Overview shows a Withholding Tax row (amount, rate, scenario) when WHT applies. This is an estimate on the project only: invoices keep their own WHT settings, typed per invoice, and are not prefilled from the project. Budget: nothing in the UI sets a project's Approved Budget any more; a budget a project already has is kept (an edit never wipes it), the API still accepts `budgetAmount`, and on production 1 of 152 projects had one. Permissions are unchanged: the tax fields follow the permissions of the project fields beside them.
- Bidding tab file areas (migration 321): the Bidding Stage has four sub-tabs, in this order: Technical Bid, Commercial Bid, **Cost Analysis**, Clarifications Bid. Each is a plain file area where people with edit access upload files (any type, up to the 50 MB upload ceiling), download them and remove them; a ZIP can be opened and previewed. They share one list, one upload and one delete (`bidPackageFiles`, `uploadBidPackageFile`, `deleteBidPackageFile`, `lib/bidPackages.ts`), all on `projects.bidding.view` / `projects.bidding.edit`, and the page's per-member capability decides who sees the buttons. Cost Analysis and Commercial Bid are pricing material: both are hidden from a technical-only team member (admins, the PM, holders of `projects.edit` and commercial or no-membership viewers see them), on the page AND at the API (list, upload, delete; one flag per area in `lib/bidPackages.ts`). The Commercial builder queries of the full bid mode (`bidCostItems`, `bidSupplierQuotations`, `bidCommercialSummary`) still need only `projects.bidding.view`; all three companies run simple mode. Cost Analysis and Clarifications show their file area in simple and full bid mode (there is no builder behind them). Clarifications Bid used to be the Technical Queries screen (`project_tqs`, gated by `projects.qaqc.*`); it was removed on 2026-11-10 with its GraphQL operations (production held 0 TQs and 0 attachments). The empty `project_tqs` table and the `tq` entity-type value are kept so a code-only rollback still works; drop them in a later migration if wanted.
- Currency of a purchase line: a PO or requisition line is stored, totalled, shown and edited in ONE currency, `po_lines.currency_code`; the page prints that column (`lib/poLineCurrency.ts`), never a guess from market or requested currency, so a row can never disagree with the Order total. A line created or added names its currency: from the form, else the one every existing line of the document shares, else the person must choose (a line is never silently put in IQD or in the base currency). Nothing is converted. Store In books a receipt's cost in the currency that cost is in (`lib/receiptCost.ts`): the base currency only for a line converted at receipt (a rate other than 1, the flow before the requisition split), otherwise the line's own currency, on the stock move and on the product's cost alike. A receipt that changes a USD product's price beyond the company tolerance therefore waits in Cost Change Approvals (before, it flipped the product to IQD). Existing lines whose stored currency is a leftover default (29 lines on 12 purchase orders on 2026-11-11) are corrected by a person, one by one, never by a bulk rule.
- Other Currency Budgets card (D2): costs booked in a currency other than the project's main budget currency can never be taken off that budget (no conversion anywhere), so each such currency gets its own card, with its own budget if an administrator added one ("+ Add Currency Budget"). It lives at the TOP of the project's Cost Control tab, above its sub-sections (`components/projects/OtherCurrencyBudgets.tsx`), no longer as a strip under the page header on every tab (moved 2026-11-10 at the user's request). Shown only to the page-wide administrator (`projects.edit`, the same rule as before, not the Cost Control edit right) and only while some currency has costs or the add form is open; so someone who is an administrator but cannot open the Cost Control tab no longer sees it.
- Invoice print attachment: a project invoice can carry ONE file, a picture (JPG, PNG) or a PDF, up to 10 MB and, for a PDF, at most 10 pages, that prints on sheets of its own AFTER the last page of the invoice (after the bank information). It is added, replaced or removed on the invoice's PDF tab by anyone holding `projects.invoices.edit`, in ANY invoice status (the user's rule: "any time", nothing locks after issue); a new file replaces the old one, and there is never more than one. It is NOT shown on the public QR verification page. It is stored as a `document_attachments` row on the invoice (entity type `project_invoice`, label `invoice_print_page`; no migration) and set only through `setInvoicePrintAttachment` / `removeInvoicePrintAttachment`, which check the permission, the company, and that the file is one the caller uploaded and nothing else uses; the generic attach routes are never used for it. A PDF is turned into one picture per page in the browser, so its text is not selectable in the print. The letterhead and stamp are not repeated on the attachment sheets, and "Page N of M" counts invoice pages only. Print / Save as PDF is held back while the attachment is loading or could not be made, so an invoice never prints without a file someone attached; an invoice with no attachment prints exactly as before.
- Cost Control tab and procurement (playbook #83): on a project's Cost Control Overview, **Actual** is the project's cost ledger (`v_project_cost_by_currency`: jobsite deliveries, stock issued, returns, posted journals) in the selected currency, plus labor and equipment entered on the tab, so it agrees with the project header; nothing is ever converted between currencies. **Committed** from a purchase order is what was ordered and NOT yet received, per line currency (ordered minus covered-from-stock minus received, at the price paid or quoted), for POs from approved to payment_pending; once goods are received the amount is Actual (jobsite) or stock (cost reaches the project when it is issued), so a purchase is never in both. "Sync PO" writes those rows (one per PO and currency, brought to 0 when the PO is received, cancelled or completed) and runs once when someone who may edit cost control opens the tab. Budget on this tab comes only from the cost codes entered on its Budget sub-tab. Open: the project header's Remaining Budget and Pending/Approved PO tiles still use older rules, and completing a jobsite PO books its whole amount again (G17).
- Cash purchases and employee advances (playbook #84): a cash purchase is paid from an employee advance, so a PO bought from the "Cash Purchase" vendor belongs on the **employee-advance flow**, not the vendor-AP flow. Finance sets the PO's funding at Payment pending (Employee advance, and which advance; the PO and the advance must be in the same currency), can **change it while the PO is still waiting** (not once a line is settled against an advance or a vendor invoice exists), assigns each line's GL Account and Cost Center, and clicks Mark as Completed: for an advance-funded PO that closes the PO and posts nothing. The lines then queue on the advance for settlement (a PO that is `completed` or `closed`), and settling books the expense by GL account and cost center. A line whose cost is already on the project (jobsite delivery) or that goes to inventory (a Store Out charges it) is settled without the project tag, so the project is never charged a second time. The vendor-AP route (Create in AP, approve, pay, then Mark as Completed) is for credit purchases from real suppliers. Still open: completing a vendor-AP PO posts its whole amount to the project again and needs an `expense_default` posting account that no company has (do not set it before that is fixed), and which advance pays which cash PO is chosen by Finance per PO.
- Advance requests (playbook #85, migration 322): an employee asks for an advance from Requisitions (Purpose: Advance: an amount, currency, optional project and the reason) and it goes the way an Expense does, with one more step. The manager approves it (`finance.advances.approve`; approving pays nothing: status `pending_issue`, "Awaiting Cash Issue"), then the cashbox handler (`finance.petty_cash.edit`) issues it from the Requisitions list or the advance page, choosing which cashbox (petty cash float) the cash leaves: it must be this company's, active, in the advance's currency, have a GL account and hold at least the amount (a hard stop, no overdraw). Issuing takes the cash out of that box, logs it as a petty-cash spend and posts a draft journal (Dr the employee's advance account, Cr the box's GL account, no project tag: handing out cash is not a project cost, settling it is); the status then becomes `approved`, which still means "issued and open for settlement" and reads "Issued". A rejected request is corrected and sent again by whoever filed it (or an admin), exactly like a rejected expense claim (`POST /finance/advances/:id/revise`, one function `canReviseAdvance`, `can_revise` on the reads). An approved request not yet paid out can be cancelled with nothing to reverse; voiding an issued one reverses the journal AND puts the cash back into its box. Everyone sees their own requests in the Requisitions list; Finance (`finance.advances.view`) sees all and opens the advance page; a cashbox handler without that permission also sees what is waiting to be issued and pays it from a dialog. No notification goes to the cashbox handler yet. The cost center is chosen by the cashbox handler in the Issue dialog (optional, the company's active ones; it replaces the advance's own only when picked), not by the approving manager, who cannot know it; settlements and expense claims settled against the advance copy it and are refused without one, and nothing sets it after the issue. Filing still needs the company's default cash account for the currency and the employee's advance account (Settings > Advance Automation).
- Document numbering (playbook #86, migration 323): each company's numbers are controlled at Settings > Company > Document Numbering (system_admin only): prefix, separator, digits, year in the number and next number, per document type. Expense Claim (`expense_claim`) is a type: default `EXP-<year>-NNNN`, one running counter like every other type (it does not restart in January by itself; reset it there), taken inside the submitting transaction (a failed submit gives the number back) and never a number already in use, so lowering the counter cannot cause a duplicate. A company with no rule keeps `EXP-<year>-NNNN` (highest of the year plus one). A type with NO rule at all gets a timestamp-style number from `nextDocumentNumber` (e.g. `ADV-1794641600903-f89d2f`): save its rule in Settings (production has the advance rules; a fresh dev database has none). Not built: creating a default rule automatically on first use.
- Petty cash float currency (playbook #89): a float has ONE currency (`petty_cash_floats.currency_code`), chosen on New Float (IQD, USD, EUR, TRY, AED; the server takes any three-letter code). It labels every journal line the float's spends, top-ups and advance issues write, and an advance can only be issued from a box in the advance's own currency. It can be corrected on Edit Float (`finance.petty_cash.edit`) only while the float holds nothing but its opening balance: no spend, no top-up request (even pending), no advance issued from it, no expense claim or payment voucher paid from it. After that it is locked (the server answers 409 `FLOAT_HAS_ACTIVITY`, the dialog shows the box disabled with the reason) and the answer is a new float in the right currency; every change is written to `audit_log`. Several floats of a company may share one cashbox GL account (production: 1211 "Main Cashbox", tagged IQD, which is also the default USD cash account); the currency of a float's lines, not the account, says what the cash is. The Petty Cash page shows one cash-held, authorized and utilization figure per currency, never added together. Two production floats created as IQD by the old form were set to USD on 2026-11-15 (Yakam "Main Cash USD", Factory "Main Cash Box USD", one audit row each); Factory's has a draft top-up journal (PCR-2026-0002) whose lines are still labelled IQD, left for Finance to decide.
- Advance-funded POs are paid per currency (playbook #90, migration 324; refines the "which advance" wording of the cash-purchase rule above): a PO funded from employee advances needs ONE advance for each currency it has lines in, chosen by Finance at Payment pending ("How was this PO paid?" > Employee advance > the employee, then one advance box per currency), never one per line. A PO with IQD and USD lines is paid from an IQD advance and a USD advance; a PO in one currency still takes one advance. The currencies are those of the LINES (`po_lines.currency_code`), not the PO header's. Each advance must be usable (approved or partially settled), this company's and in exactly the currency of its slot; nothing is ever converted. The links live in `po_funding_advances` (source of truth); `purchase_orders.funding_advance_id` holds the primary one (header currency, else first by currency) for older readers. Settlement is per line: Finance's queue for an advance lists only the lines of the POs that name it for THEIR currency, bundles in one currency settle in that currency's amounts, and the over-settlement check is against that advance's outstanding balance. A line added after funding in a currency that already has an advance uses it; in a NEW currency it has none, and completing the PO is refused (naming the currency) until Finance links one with "Change funding source". The audit record of every funding change carries the advance of each currency, before and after. The page offers one employee at a time; the server does not require both advances to belong to the same employee. The 21 mixed-currency POs waiting on 2026-11-15 were not touched; Finance funds them in the page.
- GL account and cost center of an advance settlement line (playbook #91): a settlement posts a journal line to each line's GL account, and the database refuses a header (non-postable) account, so the account must be a postable, active account of the company and the cost center an active cost center of the company. This holds for BOTH ways of settling. PO lines: the PO page's GL Account picker offers only postable accounts and the server refuses anything else (it judges only a NEW choice: a value the line already holds, sent back unchanged, passes); on the Advance page each pending line shows what is wrong with it (no GL account, header account, no cost center, not this company's), cannot be ticked until fixed, and Finance (`finance.ap.approve`, the same key as the PO page's picker) can change the account and cost center there even though the PO is closed, for lines still waiting to be settled against that advance; every change is audited. Manual "New Settlement": its form lists only postable accounts and active cost centers, creating one refuses a bad account or cost center naming each line, and approving one refuses, naming the line, only what the database would refuse (a header or foreign account, or a header account on the advance itself), so an old draft whose account was deactivated since still posts. A settlement that cannot be posted is refused before anything is written, with a plain message; the good lines of that bundle are refused with it. The 68 lines of Yakam's cash-purchase POs that carried `1000 Assets` from testing were not changed by script: Finance picks the right account per line (candidates: the postable accounts under Project Materials 3211 to 3219 and Supplies and Consumables 3251, 3254). Open: the cash-return form's Cash Account dropdown still lists the whole chart, the manual create does not check the project against the company, and the self-service settlement takes its account from the expense category.
- Open purchase orders, Committed and the project's cost ledger (playbook #92): a purchase order is FINISHED when its status is `completed`, `closed`, `cancelled` or `deleted` (one definition, `packages/db/src/poStatus.ts`); anything else is open (a `rejected` PO can be reopened, so it is open). Every count of open POs (project header and list, project detail, the completion blockers, the cancel guard, the REST completion, the company dashboard) uses it, so a project whose POs are all received and closed has none open and can be cancelled or completed. On a project's header and Cost Control, **Committed** is what was ordered and NOT yet received, per line currency, for POs from approved to payment pending (one SQL expression, `COMMITTED_LINE_VALUE_SQL`; a draft or pending-approval PO commits nothing yet), plus the store price of stock-covered lines of an OPEN PO; **Remaining Budget** is budget minus actual minus committed in one currency, and a budget of 0 means "No budget" (empty, never minus what was spent; the over-budget alert needs a budget). **Approved POs** counts POs that are approved or later and not cancelled. The Overview PO Summary lists a row per status that has POs, with the money per currency taken from the lines (never from a PO's header total, which is one number and can mix currencies), and leaves cancelled and deleted POs out of rows and total. The project's analytic account page also shows the project's **cost ledger** (`projectCostLedger`: jobsite deliveries, Store Outs, returns and posted journals, newest first, up to 500, with the PO / Store Out / return reference and totals per currency), because the cost of a project is not journal-tagged (a settlement of goods already booked is deliberately left untagged, #84b); it needs the right to see the project itself. - Store Out and stock-return cost currency (playbook #94, migration 325): a confirmed Store Out charges its project in the currency of the items issued, one cost row per currency, the currency being the product's own cost currency (`products.cost_currency`, trimmed and upper-cased; a product with none stays IQD as before, never guessed; nothing is converted). A return of stock offsets the project per currency the same way, using the same rule (`lib/stockIssueCost.ts`). So a project's Actual per currency counts a USD-priced Store Out as USD. "Actual" is what the project CONSUMED (deliveries to the jobsite plus Store Outs minus returns): goods a project PO delivers to inventory cost it nothing until a Store Out issues them, so Actual need not equal a PO total. The index on Store Out cost rows is unique on (source_id, currency_code). Rows booked before this change (92 on production, 11 projects, 3 companies, all labelled IQD) are NOT corrected by the code: they are split per project by a rehearsed SQL file after a backup, once Finance has confirmed the currency of products that look mis-tagged (the product tag is read when a row is booked or split, so a later correction does not change a booked row).
- What the project money screens count (playbook #95): the Overview's **Purchase Order Summary** is the value ORDERED on the project's POs (column "Ordered", per line currency, cancelled and deleted POs left out). The project's **cost ledger** (the analytic account page's cost cards and ledger list, the project header's Actual, the Cost Control Actual) is what has been BOOKED as cost: jobsite deliveries, Store Outs minus returns, posted journals such as expense claims; goods a project PO delivers to inventory are not a project cost until a Store Out issues them. So the two never have to match, and the difference per currency is: inventory-bound purchases, price edits after delivery (fix 3 pending), Store Outs from the central warehouse, expense claims. On a project's analytic account page the top cards show the ledger per currency and kind (`projectCostLedger.byKind`, summed in SQL over every row, not over the 500 listed); the journal table below holds only the lines TAGGED with the account (settlement journals are untagged on purpose). Any other analytic account keeps its journal totals.

- Receiving location (playbook #105): goods are received into a real location (warehouse, site, transit), never into one of the two virtual system locations, `virtual_in` "Virtual Receipts" and `virtual_out` "Virtual Consumption" (stock in Virtual Consumption counts as used up, so goods put there never become stock and a project PO delivered to inventory is never charged). The Record Receipt form does not list them, and the server refuses them when a receipt is recorded, when an old draft that names one is confirmed, and when an administrator's correction moves a receipt; with no location chosen, Store In uses the company's warehouse, else the first real active location by name, else refuses. - Equipment and returnables (playbook #107, migration 326; phase 1 of STORE_OUT_EQUIPMENT_PLAN.md): a product flagged **Equipment (must come back)** (`products.is_returnable`, optional `default_return_days`, a week when empty) is lent out when it is issued, not used up. Any other product can be ticked returnable for one line of a Store Out made in the New Store Out dialog. A returnable line costs the project nothing when it goes out, is never offered for invoicing and is not a Material Return; its stock sits in Virtual Consumption while out (no separate location) and comes back through **Inventory > Returnables > Receive back** (`createEquipmentReturn`): quantity, condition (good, needs repair, damaged: back into a real location of the company that holds it; lost: not returned) and who received it. A lost quantity is written off and charged to the Store Out's project at the line's cost as stock issued, unless it was a loan. A manual Store Out with equipment must say who it is issued to (an employee, or a typed name for a contractor) and may carry a department; a Store Out linked to a PO is exempt. Equipment is tracked by quantity, with an optional serial note on the line. An issued Store Out that handed out equipment cannot be cancelled. **Loan:** equipment held by the central warehouse may be issued to another company's person or project with no price, no inter-company transfer and no bill; only the lending company takes it back, the lender never sees the borrower's project, and a lost loan is a write-off in the lender's books only (no project is charged; management decides any recharge). Mobile: the REST issue route refuses equipment. Phases 2 and 3 (temporary stores, transfers in transit, one history list) are planned, not built.

- Temporary stores and store transfers (playbook #108, migration 327; phase 2 of STORE_OUT_EQUIPMENT_PLAN.md): a location may be a **temporary store** (Settings of Inventory > Locations, type Temporary): a real place where bought goods wait until they are sent on. Receiving goods into one needs a **final destination** (a warehouse or site of the same company, saved on the receipt); no other receiving location takes one. **Inventory > Temp Store & Transit** lists what waits in the temporary stores (store, supplier and PO, destination, since when), flags anything waiting more than 7 days, and from it ticked items go on as a **store transfer**: from one store to another of the SAME company, with driver, vehicle, waybill and reference. Sending takes the stock out of the From store at once (it must be available, what other documents reserved does not count) into the company's transit location, where it stays until the other end **receives** it: each item is received in full or short (a short item needs a remark, and what did not arrive is written off to Virtual Consumption with that remark, status "Received short"). A transfer still in transit can be cancelled and everything goes back. Goods are never received INTO the transit location. A transfer between two companies is not this: it stays an inter-company transfer. The instant manual transfer (Stock Moves > Transfer) is unchanged. Phase 3 (one history list) is planned, not built.

- Store History (playbook #109; phase 3 of STORE_OUT_EQUIPMENT_PLAN.md): **Inventory > Store History** is one list of everything that moved stock through a store: Store In (goods receipts), Store Out, store transfers and equipment returns, newest first (the newest 500; the page says when it was cut). Filter by kind, by store (a document matches a store if it touched it in any way: receiving place or final destination, shelf taken from, either end of a transfer, place equipment went back to) and by a search over the document number, the supplier / person / destination and the reference. It is read-only and adds nothing up. Store In rows follow the same branch visibility as the Store In list. Clicking opens the Store In or Store Out page, the transfer's own details window on Temp Store & Transit for a transfer (cancelled ones too; the address is `?transfer=<id>`), or Returnables for a return. On Temp Store & Transit a click on a received transfer opens the same details window: both stores, dates, who received it, driver, vehicle, waybill, reference, notes, and every item sent against received and short, with both remarks. This completes the three phases of the Store Out / equipment plan.
